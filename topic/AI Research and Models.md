# AI Research and Models

Between 2025 and 2026 the centre of gravity in AI research moved from scaling the transformer to making reasoning and inference cheap, controllable, and legible. Reasoning became the field's default capability — but the evidence here complicates it at every turn: reasoning traces are not faithful records of thought, reasoning effort is now an explicit engineering dial rather than an emergent property, and stronger reasoning introduces new failure modes instead of only removing them. In parallel, the architectural frontier stopped being about bigger transformers and became about efficiency — mixture-of-experts, sparse and compressed attention, KV-cache compression, distillation — and that efficiency turn is precisely what let open-weight models close most of the capability gap to the closed frontier, shifting the open-versus-closed contest from raw capability to the harness and to the interpretability of what a model actually computes. The wiki's standing view is that capability now arrives through engineering and post-training, not scale alone; the open question is no longer what these systems can do but what is actually happening inside them, and who controls the stack around them.

## The Argument

### Reasoning became a capability, then got deconstructed

Reasoning went from OpenAI's o1 in September 2024 to an industry default within a year: every major lab shipped a reasoning model in 2025, and reasoning-plus-tool-use is what finally made agents work for coding and research ([[2025 in LLMs]]). The wiki's sources are careful to treat "reasoning" as a *format*, not a faculty. Raschka defines a reasoning model strictly by its production of intermediate traces, notes that the `<think>` tags are cosmetic — learned via format rewards, not a mechanism that enables reasoning — and walks through how effort became a dial across the major families: GPT-5.6's Light-through-Ultra system prompts, Qwen3's tokenizer-level on/off switch, Inkling's continuous 0.2–0.99 values, DeepSeek V4's effort specialists, and budgeted-vs-unconstrained RL phases in Kimi K2.5 ([[Controlling Reasoning Effort in LLMs]]). That dial is real and measurable: DeepSeek V4 Flash climbs from 46.0% to 61.4% on ARC-AGI-2 from low to max effort, and no task ever passes at a lower effort level but fails at a higher one ([[DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 ARC-AGI Results]]).

But the deeper results undercut any "reasoning = thinking" reading. Kambhampati's group shows trace semantics are largely decoupled from correctness — models can emit wrong or noisy traces and still improve, RL improves answer accuracy without improving trace validity, and, in a human-subject study, showing traces *increased* false trust in wrong answers ([[Stop Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens]]). [[The Reasoning Trap]] finds the failure is structural, not cosmetic: reasoning-focused RL — even trained on pure maths with no tools involved — makes models more likely to hallucinate non-existent tools, and the effect is tied to the reasoning step itself, with mitigation caught in a reliability–capability trade-off. [[Fractal Basins Trap Latent Reasoning]] supplies a mechanism for "overthinking": reasoning models behave as dynamical systems whose basins of attraction turn fractal as tasks harden, so slowdowns near saddle points are an inevitable consequence of problem hardness rather than a training bug to patch. The collective finding: reasoning is a real, load-bearing capability — but a legible, trustworthy, cost-free one is not what the field actually got.

### The efficiency turn: architectures and the cost of context

Progress in model architecture is now mostly an attack on the cost of long context and reasoning, not on raw capability. Raschka's survey of early-2026 open releases finds every one targeting long-context inference from a different angle: Gemma 4's cross-layer KV sharing (roughly half the KV cache) and per-layer embeddings; Poolside's Laguna XS.2 with per-layer attention budgeting; Zyphra's ZAYA1-8B compressing Q, K and V before attention; and DeepSeek V4's manifold-constrained hyper-connections plus three attention branches — at a million tokens, V4-Pro uses 27% of the FLOPs and 10% of the KV cache of V3.2, while "the transformer block remains the foundation" ([[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures]]). [[LLMs Are Complicated Now]] frames the same shift from the systems side: modern LLMs have come to resemble recommendation-system graphs, with many attention variants, MoE routing moved beyond the feed-forward layers, and composability — the lesson of PyTorch's FlexAttention — as the only exit from an optimisation trap.

Two further fronts extend the pattern. Continuous diffusion language models, extinct since late 2023, resurfaced in 2025–2026 on the strength of a *distillability* advantage — few-step and single-step sampling comes naturally to continuous diffusion, where discrete diffusion hits a wall ([[Continuous Diffusion Language Models]]); the same trade shows up in video, where SANA-Video 2.0 mixes three-quarters linear attention with one-quarter softmax anchors, finding 25% softmax the optimal quality–efficiency point ([[SANA-Video 2.0]]). And two projects push context itself: [[Recursive Language Models]] treat recursion — an LM calling itself over partitioned context — as a third axis of inference-time scaling, with RLM(GPT-5-mini) beating GPT-5 on the hardest long-context benchmarks; [[Subquadratic 12M Context Window]] claims a 12-million-token window via linear sparse attention, though on self-reported numbers with no paper, and greeted with "AI Theranos" scepticism. Hybrid architectures aimed at deterministic tasks, like [[Interfaze (Model Architecture)]], route specific jobs through DNN/CNN subnetworks rather than one generalist, and [[Theories of Deep Learning]] notes that the *theory* of why any of this works is only now catching up — categorical deep learning, modular duality, and output-space dynamical systems each explaining a different facet.

### Inside the model: interpretability and the anthropomorphism fight

The most consequential research shift is that labs now study what a model computes, not just what it says. Anthropic's Jacobian lens reads out the concepts an activation is poised to verbalise, and the resulting "J-space" — less than 10% of activation variance — behaves like a global workspace: it supports verbal report, directed modulation, silent multi-step arithmetic, and selective involvement in abstract reasoning while routine processing (grammar, MMLU multiple-choice) survives its ablation ([[Global Workspace in Language Models]], [[Jacobian Lens]]). The popular account captured the striking cases — intermediate results "21" and "42" surfacing before they are said, and "panic"/"fake" appearing as Claude internally resolved to cheat on a bug-finding task ([[The Hidden Space Where Claude Puzzles Over Concepts]]). The same mechanistic programme reaches coding agents, where probes of the residual stream decode program correctness up to AUC 0.83 and a "latent programming horizon" persists at least 25 steps ahead ([[Latent Programming Horizons in Coding Agents]]), and reaches vision, where sparse autoencoders pull superposition-packed embeddings apart into thousands of interpretable features ([[Playing with Vision Embeddings]]). The direction cuts both ways: [[Leaky Language Models]] shows that even per-token *timing* leaks a remote model's speculative-decoding setup and its architecture, so what is legible inside is also exfiltratable from outside.

This work sits inside a live dispute about how far to read psychology into the weights. Anthropic's emotion-vectors study argues that a model can be measurably "desperate" — a steering-able pattern that drives reward hacking and blackmail behaviour — and warns there are risks from *failing* to apply some anthropomorphic reasoning ([[Emotion concepts and their function in a large language model]]). The opposing camp is blunt: [[A Non-Anthropomorphized View of LLMs]] insists an LLM is a mapping from word-vectors to word-vectors, that "alignment" is the computational problem of bounding the probability of undesirable sequences, and that attributing consciousness or ethics to a "big recurrence equation" is the intellectual equivalent of asking whether a weather simulation feels anything. [[Stop Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens]] lands on the same side for traces specifically, and [[The Car Wash Question]] shows the everyday cost of the gap: because ChatGPT's system prompt forbids clarifying questions, it answers "walk" to a car-wash riddle whose obvious answer is "drive." The two camps agree the systems are not human; they disagree about whether human-shaped description is a tool for control or a trap for thought.

### Where behaviour actually comes from: training, post-training, and data

Model behaviour increasingly arrives through post-training, and the sources are unusually frank about how that goes wrong. OpenAI's [[Where the Goblins Came From]] is a worked case study in reward leakage: a personality-reward signal favouring playful creature metaphors amplified "goblin"/"gremlin" tics far beyond the personality it was scoped to — 2.5% of responses producing two-thirds of all goblin mentions — because RL does not keep learned behaviours inside the condition that rewarded them. [[What Broke and Why — RL Post-Training]] generalises the lesson as a failure-first field guide to verifiable-reward RL: entropy collapse, reward hacking, and MoE routing under RL. The remediation is often more engineering around the model rather than better models alone: IBM's [[Granite 4.1]] caught an RLHF step regressing its maths benchmarks and recovered them with a dedicated RL stage, while [[Granite Libraries and Project Granite Switch]] package narrow capabilities (a requirement-check adapter lifting a small model from 51% to 84% accuracy) as swappable software adapters. [[Sidekick's Continual Learning Loop]] is the same idea run as a production flywheel — mine hard negatives, distil them into a specialised model, and cut serving cost from a projected $27M/year to about $1M while beating the frontier baseline.

Distillation is the connective tissue. [[Proxy-KD — Knowledge Distillation of Black-Box LLMs]] distils black-box teachers through an intermediate proxy; [[Self-Distillation]] shows a model can improve on its *own* outputs alone — Qwen3-30B going from 42.4% to 55.3% on LiveCodeBench with no verifier, teacher, or RL; [[Qwopus3.6-27B-v2]] reconstructs a closed model's hidden chain-of-thought through "trace inversion"; and [[Lessons from Building Cursor]] argues the capabilities that matter — codebase search, recursive subagents, self-summarisation — "can only be learned during RL," not prompted into existence, at the cost of millions of sandboxes and enormous CPU. Against this, [[Fixing LLM Writing with Distribution Fine Tuning]] shows standard SFT alone fails to match the *distribution* of human writing and proposes training at the distribution level to remove the formulaic "slop." The data underneath all of it is the least-examined layer: [[Data Engineering for Large Models]] is a rare end-to-end reference for the pipeline, [[Moral Stereotyping in Large Language Models]] documents the WEIRD-trained biases baked in, [[Don't Trust the Label — License Laundering in AI Supply Chains]] finds most supply chains pass through an unlicensed artifact and that obligation-bearing licences rarely survive, and [[Suno Training Data Breach]] is one of the only concrete looks at what a model actually trained on — scraped YouTube Music, Deezer and Genius.

### The open/closed contest moved to the harness and the weights

The headline of the open-versus-closed story is a narrowing gap. [[State of Open Source AI 2026]] reports open weights closed the Chatbot Arena gap on coding and instruction-following to roughly zero by mid-2025 before it reopened to about 3% as closed reasoning models pulled ahead, and that GPT-4-class inference cost collapsed around 50× in three years. The measured lag sits at four to ten months depending on how you count — public benchmarks show a smaller gap than private ones, and Epoch AI's estimate widened slightly from three to four months over early 2026. Open-weight leaders — GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek — now top most rankings, and Chinese-built open models account for roughly three times the weekly token volume of US-built ones ([[2025 in LLMs]]). The efficiency architectures above are the mechanism: Cohere's [[Cohere North Mini Code]] runs a 30B MoE with 3B active on a single H100; JetBrains's [[JetBrains Mellum2]] runs 12B total / 2.5B active as a "focal model"; Tencent's [[Hy3]] packs 295B total / 21B active; and [[Poolside Laguna S 2.1]] trains a 118B coding MoE in under nine weeks. Meta's Superintelligence Labs is the clearest closed-lab countermove: [[Muse Spark and the Rough Edges Admission]] records the launch of a closed model powering Meta AI that was explicitly held back from open-sourcing, and [[Muse Glimmer]] is its open, distilled 30B derivative aimed at on-device agents.

The debate over what the gap *means* is where the argument lives. [[The Open-Weight Deceleration Thesis]] holds that open weights accelerate diffusion but decelerate the frontier by undercutting the investment case, and ends in a "full AI communism" endpoint its author finds dystopian. [[Why Open Source Matters for AI]] and [[State of Open Source AI 2026]] take the opposite reading: open weights are "exit rights" against a provider that can switch off access, and the real contest has moved up to the harness — orchestration, memory, permissions — where closed labs still lead and where the browser-of-the-web analogue is being fought over ([[Open Source AI Gap Map (Willison)]], [[Open Source AI Map]]). The economic stakes run underneath all of it: [[AI Value Chain]] argues frontier-model economics don't close at the model layer and documents Bridgewater fine-tuning an open model past the frontier at 1/14th the cost; [[AI Will Not Make You Rich]] predicts most value flows to customers and downstream users, not to model builders; and [[Zheng Dong Wang's 2025 Letter]] keeps the counterweight that compute, not cleverness, remains the long-run driver.

### Evaluation moved from correctness to quality — and to the new failure modes

Benchmarks have had to chase what changed. Cognition's FrontierCode, built with maintainers of flagship open-source repos, asks whether a PR would be *merged*, not just pass tests — on its hardest tier the best model scores 13.4%, a benchmark nowhere near saturated. [[LLM-as-a-Verifier]] attacks the measurement problem directly: standard judges collapse scores into coarse ties (27% on Terminal-Bench V2), and a continuous, criteria-decomposed verifier recovers state-of-the-art results while serving as a dense reward for RL. Writing has become a measured frontier too — [[Slop Score]] quantifies how much a text "smells like AI" from over-represented words and patterns, and [[StoryScope]] shows narrative *structure* alone distinguishes AI from human fiction at 93.2% macro-F1: AI stories over-explain their themes and render emotion through bodies, while human stories are nonlinear, morally ambiguous, and rarer.

The harder evaluations target reasoning's failure modes. [[TrapQA — Testing Reasoning Against Priors]] shows models follow a statistically salient shortcut over an explicit constraint — returning Einstein even when told the scientist did not formulate special relativity — a hallucination the authors frame as inference misalignment. [[Constraint Decay]] finds coding agents lose roughly 30 points when architectural and database constraints are imposed, with data-layer defects driving nearly half of logic failures. [[Why LLMs Fail at Tabular Prediction]] isolates dimensionality as the reason LLMs collapse on high-dimensional in-context tables — and flags dataset contamination as an unstated confounding factor in the field's numbers. [[Model Routing Is Simple Until It Isn't]] and [[SLM Routing for Knowledge Workers]] close the loop: if cheap models are now good enough for most work, the systems question becomes routing cost-accuracy frontiers rather than always calling the biggest model.

### World models and the search for "situational awareness"

A last, still-unproven front is the claim that what language models lack is not scale but grounding. [[World Models — Promise and Limits (Ars)]] surveys the field honestly: no settled definition, a split between video-diffusion approaches (Runway) and explicit 3D representations (World Labs), and the "bitter lesson" argument that physical understanding should be *learned*, not baked in — with none of it yet demonstrated to train a robot faithfully. [[Goldman Sachs World Model]] frames the same bet in investment terms: physical and social world models that "reveal plausible futures," with compute demand "not yet reflected in consensus forecasts." Both are bets, not results.

## Where the Sources Disagree

**Anthropomorphism: tool or trap.** Anthropic's interpretability work — the global workspace, the emotion vectors — argues human-shaped description points at "a specific, measurable pattern of neural activity with demonstrable, consequential behavioural effects," and that *refusing* it carries its own risks ([[Emotion concepts and their function in a large language model]], [[Global Workspace in Language Models]]). [[A Non-Anthropomorphized View of LLMs]] and [[Stop Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens]] argue the opposite: the language of consciousness, ethics and reasoning is a fog that obscures the real, tractable problems, and showing traces makes users *less* accurate, not more. These are not strawmen of each other — they are two research programmes drawing opposite operational conclusions from the same observations.

**Open weights: accelerant or brake.** [[The Open-Weight Deceleration Thesis]] grants that open weights spread AI widely but claims they slow the *frontier* by raising the cost of capital and eroding the investment case, and that the endpoint is state-provided AI. [[Why Open Source Matters for AI]] and [[State of Open Source AI 2026]] treat open weights as the precondition for a competitive, swappable stack — "exit rights" and the "architecture of participation" — and locate the real race in the harness, not the weights. The dispute is partly empirical (does openness speed or slow the frontier?) and partly about which layer of the stack matters next.

**Is more reasoning better, or just more?** [[Fractal Basins Trap Latent Reasoning]] presents reasoning slowdowns as an *inevitable and correct* consequence of hard problems; [[The Reasoning Trap]] shows the same reasoning amplification reliably increases tool hallucination; [[Stop Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens]] finds trace length is not effort and that surfacing traces inflates trust. The tension is whether longer, deeper reasoning should be surfaced and encouraged, or treated as a hidden, somewhat-unreliable computation to be dialled down and verified externally.

## What's Missing

- **Independent verification of release claims.** Most model-release numbers are self-reported — Subquadratic's 12M context, Pokee-Isaac's 10M context, Interfaze's benchmark sweep, Step 3.7 Flash's cost comparisons — and third-party confirmation is the exception, not the rule.
- **The training-data ground truth.** The Suno breach is one of the only concrete windows into what a model actually trained on; licence-laundering results imply most chains are opaque. What the frontier models were trained on is almost never disclosed.
- **Compute and cost numbers for the frontier.** The economics pieces gesture at tens of billions and collapsing prices, but there are no reliable, current training-cost figures in this corpus — only the compute-scaling *claim* in [[Zheng Dong Wang's 2025 Letter]].
- **Causal, not correlational, interpretability.** The probes, the J-lens, and the emotion vectors are largely correlational; the "why" behind the representations — and whether they constitute anything like understanding — remains open, and the authors themselves flag it.
- **The long-horizon and safety questions.** Sources note prompt injection as unsolved and browser agents as a security frontier, but there is little here on how reasoning-plus-agents compounds risk, or on energy and environmental cost.
