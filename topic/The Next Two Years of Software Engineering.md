# The Next Two Years of Software Engineering

Addy Osmani's survey of the macro forces reshaping software engineering. The headline finding: a Harvard study shows junior developer employment drops 9-10% within six quarters of AI adoption, while senior roles stay stable. But the piece resists doomerism -- the BLS still projects 15% growth in software jobs through 2034, and non-tech industries may absorb what Big Tech sheds. The real question isn't whether jobs disappear but whether the *nature* of the work changes beyond recognition.

---

## Key Quotes

> "The best software engineers won't be the fastest coders, but those who know when to distrust AI."

> "I don't want to end up as a code janitor, cleaning up what the AI throws over the wall."

> "Engineers become 'composers,' orchestrating ensembles of AI agents."

## Key Themes

#future-of-work #junior-developers #skills-atrophy #t-shaped #education #role-evolution

The "distrust AI" quote is the most important sentence in the piece. It reframes the value proposition: expertise isn't about what you can build, it's about what you can catch. That connects to [[Fresh Eyes]] (using different models to catch blind spots), [[AI PR Reviewer]] (automated catching), and [[AI Zealotry]]'s emphasis on tests over code reading.

The T-shaped developer argument is sound but not new. What's new is the data: 45% of engineering roles now expect multi-domain proficiency. The specialist-generalist question was always relevant; AI just accelerated it.

## Critical Analysis

This is a well-researched survey piece, but it hedges so carefully that it's hard to extract a strong take. "Multiple futures are possible" and "reality will combine elements" is true but not useful for decision-making. The strongest section is on education -- the observation that 45% of companies are dropping bachelor's degree requirements signals a real structural shift.

The companion piece ([[Addy Osmani's Workflow]]) is more actionable. This one is better as context-setting. The tension between the two is revealing: the workflow piece assumes human oversight as non-negotiable, while this piece acknowledges futures where that assumption breaks down. See [[Five Levels from Spicy Autocomplete to the Dark Software Factory]] for a framework that makes the Level 3-4 transition explicit, and [[Two Kinds of User Are Emerging]] for the enterprise adoption angle.
