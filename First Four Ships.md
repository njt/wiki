# First Four Ships

The 1850 organized settlement of Christchurch, New Zealand, executed by the Canterbury Association from 12,000 miles away with six-month communication latency — a case study in spec-first planning, infrastructure-before-people execution, and how early design decisions compound into permanent social structure.

---

## Key Quotes

> "The Canterbury Association's inaugural meeting took place in London on 27 March 1848. A resolution passed at that meeting gave the settlement the name 'Canterbury' and the chief town 'Christchurch.'"

Two guys in a London meeting room name a city that will exist on the other side of the planet. The entire thing is a spec document with a six-month feedback loop. If they got it wrong, the correction wouldn't arrive until after another wave of ships had already departed.

> "When Godley arrived in April 1850, he halted all work due to lack of funds."

The man on the ground overruled the plan. This is the 1850 equivalent of an engineer looking at the architecture doc and saying "we can't build this on the current budget." Remote planning is necessary; local authority is non-negotiable.

> "Each ship carried a chaplain, a doctor, and a schoolmaster."

The Canterbury Association wasn't just shipping people — they were shipping a society. Every vessel was a self-contained seed of institutional civilization. You don't get a second chance to retrofit culture onto a colony, so you bake it into the manifest.

> "Cabin passage cost £42, cheaper berths £25, and steerage £15."

Three-tier pricing encoded the class structure of Christchurch from day zero. The families who could afford cabin passage are, in several cases, still prominent. The economic gradient of a city was set by a fare schedule written in a London office.

## Key Themes

- #concept **Spec-first settlement**: The Canterbury Association planned Christchurch like a software project — surveys, town plans, infrastructure — before the "users" arrived
- #concept **Infrastructure-before-people**: Joseph Thomas and his team built jetties, roads, and the Bridle Path before the first settler ship docked. The harness matters more than the passengers
- #concept **Latency-tolerant planning**: Six months between decision and feedback. Every instruction had to be complete and correct because you couldn't iterate
- #person **Edward Gibbon Wakefield** — The architect of systematic colonization, applying industrial planning logic to settlement
- #person **John Robert Godley** — The on-the-ground authority who wasn't afraid to halt the entire project when the numbers didn't work

## Critical Analysis

The "First Four Ships" story is told in New Zealand as founding mythology — the *Mayflower* of the South Island. What's left out is instructive. The Canterbury Association was a property speculation scheme dressed in Anglican vestments. "Systematic colonization" meant buying land cheap from Māori (or simply taking it), selling it dear to English gentry, and using the margin to ship labourers who'd work for the landowners. The chaplain-doctor-schoolmaster per ship isn't benevolence; it's a retention strategy for the paying customers.

The article itself is weak — Wikipedia flags it for inadequate citations, and the "Notable Passengers" section is just a template dump of names with no narrative. The interesting story isn't the ships; it's the planning apparatus that launched them. The Canterbury Association's real innovation was logistical: how do you deploy a functional town to a site you've never seen, using only written instructions and people you can't call back?

The six-month latency is the detail that should stop you. Every decision made in London between 1848 and 1850 was irreversible in practice. If the survey was wrong, if the site was flood-prone, if the Māori had objections — too late, the ships had sailed. This is [[Specifications as the Product]] at civilizational scale. The spec is the settlement; the settlers are just the runtime.

The class stratification encoded in ticket pricing makes me think of [[Prefix Effects]] — an early naming or pricing decision that creates gravity, shaping everything built on top of it. Christchurch's social structure was priced in before anyone set foot on shore.

Godley's willingness to halt work — burning political capital, embarrassing the Association, delaying the entire enterprise — is the kind of decision that only gets made by someone with skin in the game. Remote committees optimize for momentum; the person on the ground optimizes for survival. This is the dynamic that [[How I've Run Major Projects]] describes as "detailed planning" meeting "fast OODA loops."
