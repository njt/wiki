# AstaBrief-8B

Ai2's open-weight Qwen3-8B fine-tune (SFT + offline DPO) that converts a research question and retrieved literature excerpts into a cited report — a deliberate distillation of its own multi-agent Asta ScholarQA pipeline into a single small model. Trained on reports generated by frontier models (Claude, o3/o4-mini, GPT-4.1, DeepSeek) and DPO-ranked by LLM judges validated against humans at 95% agreement, it reaches 87.0 average on ScholarQA-CS2 and wins 72% of head-to-heads against Asta ScholarQA itself on the test set.

---

## What the card actually documents

This is a model card, not an essay, but it is a quietly important one. Three things are documented precisely:

- **The distillation recipe.** Candidate reports come from *two* architectures — multi-step ScholarQA and single-step generation — across seven backing models. The DPO data (AstaBrief_DPO_Mix) is pairs of real-query reports with judge-chosen winners; only pairs where both judges (GPT-4.1 and DeepSeek-R1) agreed survived, after measuring 95% judge–human agreement.
- **The metrics, with citations separated.** The eval reports ingredient recall, answer precision, citation precision, and citation recall as distinct axes. DPO moves citation recall from 71.3 to 78.2 while answer precision slightly *drops* (90.4 → 89) — a trade the card shows rather than hides.
- **The reproducibility details.** open-instruct, 8×H100, 7 epochs, DPO-Norm, β=10, 16k context, BF16. Apache 2.0 on Qwen3-8B.

The card also warns bluntly: use the exact fine-tuning prompt format or expect "degraded or inconsistent behavior."

## Why it matters

- **The research-pipeline-as-data move.** Asta ScholarQA is a multi-agent system; Ai2 ran it at scale, kept the outputs, and used them as preference data. The pipeline is not replaced — it becomes the teacher. That's a pattern with legs: agent *trajectories* are more valuable than agent *deployments*.
- **Judges all the way down.** The preference data is judge-generated, and the judges are themselves validated against humans before being trusted. This is the mature form of LLM-as-judge — validate first, then gate.
- **Small models closing on pipelines.** An 8B model matching (and head-to-head beating) a full agentic pipeline is the strongest recent data point that deep research is compressible.

## Opinionated take

The honest reading is "distillation works on tasks with checkable structure." Cited literature reports are the ideal substrate: citations are verifiable, preference judgments over report pairs are well-defined, and the eval (ScholarQA-CS2) is concrete. The card's own numbers nuance the victory lap — DeepScholarBench (53.50) trails ScholarQA (60.25) and DR-Tulu (56.26), so the win is benchmark-dependent, not universal. The 72% win rate on SQA-CS2 test sits next to a 55% on dev; anyone citing this should quote both. What's genuinely established is that one 8B forward pass is *in the conversation* with a multi-agent system, at trivially lower serving cost — and the model card is transparent about the trade surface (citation recall up, answer precision down) rather than selling a single headline.

## Themes

#concept #tool

- **Distillation of agentic pipelines into single models** — the teacher is a system, not just a bigger model.
- **Validated LLM-as-judge** — 95% human agreement measured *before* judge labels were trusted.
- **Citations as a first-class metric** — recall and precision on citations tracked separately from answer quality.
