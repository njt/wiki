# HUMAN.md — A Manifesto for Human Contribution in the AI Era

Briana (Mazzio) May's HUMAN.md (April 2026) is a ten-point working manifesto on what humans actually bring to creative work once the machines are "very good and getting better every day." Written as notes-to-self rather than a thinkpiece, it argues that "taste" is too small a word for the human contribution: the real work is deliberate rule-breaking, human advocacy, emotional resonance, full ownership of output, and refusing to be a spectator to your own generation.

---

## What it argues

The manifesto's ten principles: (1) break the rule on purpose, in public, with a reason — "if you can't say *why* you broke it, you didn't break it, you just missed"; (2) find inspiration in weird places, since "a model averages the internet" while a human walks somewhere strange; (3) advocate for the human and humanity at every brief; (4) design for emotional resonance, not correctness — "correct" is "the lowest bar in the building"; (5) seek delight over delivery; (6) experiment ruthlessly and ship more than you opine — "make 50 things, throw away 47, talk about the 3"; (7) own the output fully; (8) design *with* the machine, not next to it; (9) play with purpose and for play; (10) be both a builder and an ideas person, because "the split is fake."

## Key quotes

> "If the AI output is bad, the AI did not fail you. *You* failed it. You picked the tool. You wrote the prompt. You accepted the result. You shipped it."

Total ownership, no outsourced blame. This is the manifesto's spine: the gap between what you wanted and what came back is "almost always a gap in *you*, not in the tool."

> "AI is not the great equalizer, it is the great amplifier."

A crisp refutation of the democratisation narrative. Powerful tools reward the people who showed up sharp and expose the people who didn't — which complicates any story where models flatten skill differences.

> "If you're a spectator to your own output, you are not the author of your own output. Get your hands in."

The work is the loop — the push, the hundredth iteration — not the first prompt and its acceptance.

> "This file will be wrong in six months. That is fine. I'd rather be specifically wrong than vaguely correct."

An unusual closing honesty for a manifesto: provisional by design, dated like a config file (the title itself is a file — human.md answering the machine's CLAUDE.md-style documents).

## Themes

#concept (the human engine beneath taste; AI as amplifier) #pattern (design *with* the model in the loop; own-the-output accountability) #culture (ship over opine; play with and without purpose)

## Analysis

This is the creative-practitioner's mirror image of the agent-engineering literature in this wiki, and it lands better than most of it precisely because it isn't a thinkpiece — it's a contract with oneself. The "great amplifier" line is the sharpest claim here: it reframes model capability as exposing variance in human preparation rather than compressing it, which cuts against both the doom reading (humans made redundant) and the hype reading (everyone's a creator now). The "spectator" principle is essentially the same argument the coding side makes about verification and engagement — passive acceptance of generated output is where authorship dies — arriving from a design direction with none of the engineering vocabulary.

Where it's weak: it gestures at "the engine underneath taste" but never names what that engine is beyond advocacy and intent, and principles like "advocate for humanity" risk being unactionable defaults. But the piece pre-empts this — it's explicitly notes-to-self, and its willingness to be "specifically wrong" is the point.
