# Harvard CS 2881R — Challenges in AI Alignment and Safety

Boaz Barak's Fall 2026 Harvard graduate seminar on AI alignment and safety is a syllabus-page source, but its format is the interesting part: 13 mandatory in-person lectures with frontier-lab guest speakers (Altman, Schulman, Pachocki, Carlini, Lambert, Toner, Cotra), Perusall pre-reading discussions, and — unusually — a weekly student-run experiment probing a live question about LLM behaviour. It also encourages full agentic AI use for coursework and openly discloses the instructor's OpenAI role.

---

The seminar's student experiments read like a micro-catalogue of what the community is actually worried about in late 2026: whether chain-of-thought is an interchangeable scratchpad, whether a small model can spot a large model's cyberattack in service logs, filler-token effects on reasoning, whether agents can collaborate on ML research, RL environment quality across self-play, refusal geometry and political bias, evaluation awareness (the Hawthorne effect for evals), and neuron interpretability via "WeirdChat". That is empirical safety research scaled down to a semester and handed to students.

Guest lineup is the other signal. Between OpenAI's chief scientist (Pachocki) and its former researcher Schulman, an OpenAI conflict-of-interest disclosure sits alongside Anthropic's Carlini, METR's Cotra, Georgetown's Toner, philosopher Jeff Sebo, and forecasters Kokotajlo and Dwarkesh. The course treats alignment as spanning interpretability, RL, forecasting, policy, and moral status — one seminar, all lanes.

Barak's own framing is candid: "I (Boaz) will see it as a great success of the course if its graduates work in AI safety in any capacity, including in academia, nonprofits, governments, or any of OpenAI's competitors."

## Key quotes

> Submitting Homework Zero is a **necessary but not sufficient** condition for admission to the course.

Selection by demonstrated effort before the first lecture — a gate that filters for motivation in a field where most interest is performative.

> Students are allowed and encouraged to use generative and agentic AI as much as they can for studying, exploring concepts, and completing their assignments and projects.

A safety course that mandates tool use rather than banning it; the meta-lesson is that studying AI safety with agents is itself an alignment-adjacent experiment.

> In addition to his position at Harvard, Boaz is also a member of the technical staff at OpenAI.

An unusually direct conflict disclosure, and a success criterion that treats a lab's competitors as equally valid destinations for safety work.

## Take

The syllabus is mostly unfilled placeholders — the readings and materials sections are empty — so what you are ingesting is the skeleton: the speaker list, the experiment titles, and the course policies. Even so, the skeleton is diagnostic. The weekly student experiments are the standout: safety education here is not canonical reading plus essays, it is running small, well-scoped empirical probes on real models. That mirrors how interpretability and evals research actually happens, and it argues that alignment pedagogy should train experiment design, not just crisis framing. The framing readings — "It's 2030 and we fucked up" — push the opposite, catastrophe-first instinct; the tension between those two registers is the course's real design question. The OpenAI conflict disclosure, handled with unusual directness, is also worth noting as governance practice: name the conflict, state the success metric that isn't captured by it, and move on.
