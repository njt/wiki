# Agent Building Playbook (Ramparte)

Ramparte's single-page registry of ~100 compressed patterns for building agentic systems, each a named aphorism with topic tags, filterable by dimension. It is less an essay than a field taxonomy — the accumulated operating moves of agentic practice, packaged the way the playbook itself says practice should be packaged.

---

## What it argues

The page's form is its argument: agentic practice has matured enough to be catalogued. Each pattern is one sentence of dense advice — "Fail Loud Over Fallbacks", "Separate the Proposer From the Authority", "Agents Amplify Experience" — tagged across thirteen dimensions. The recurring spine is that context, attention, and verification — not model capability — are the actual bottlenecks of agentic work.

## Key quotes

> "Every context management decision is one of four moves — write it out, select only the relevant part, compress the verbose into the essential, or isolate the detail into a sub-agent."

A genuinely good compression. Most context-engineering advice collapses into these four verbs; having them named makes delegation decisions legible.

> "An agent's claim of success is a hypothesis — treat it as one until you hold evidence that no longer originates from the agent itself."

The sharpest line in the registry. It is the epistemological core of the whole verification cluster: nothing an agent says about its own work counts as knowledge.

> "Every degree of autonomy you give an agent must be paid for with oversight proportional to the risk."

Autonomy as a priced currency, not a dial to max out. This is the governance pattern the whole fleet-operations literature keeps rediscovering.

> "Periodically have a stronger model inspect your workflow and delete steps that are no longer needed... because model capability changes faster than workflow habits, and yesterday's scaffolding becomes today's tax."

The meta-pattern that makes the rest durable — it anticipates its own obsolescence, which is rare in a genre that usually presents itself as timeless.

## Take

The registry's strength is also its limit: 100 one-liners with no worked examples, no failure stories, no numbers. It names the moves but doesn't show the cost of getting them wrong — which matters, because several of its best patterns (brownfield regression-first, don't automate the workaround, the agent's struggle as a maintainability signal) are exactly the ones you only believe after the expensive incident. As a checklist it's excellent; as a curriculum it's a table of contents. Its honest self-description — "the package manager of agentic practice" — is right: it indexes practice, it doesn't teach it.

## Relations

This source is a direct catalogue counterpart to [[Nine Harness Patterns (deepsense.ai)]], which curates a much smaller set with more depth per pattern — the playbook is breadth where that is depth, and the two overlap heavily on verification and context patterns. It strengthens [[The State of Context Engineering (Reunig)]]'s thesis with a vocabulary: the four moves (write, select, compress, isolate) are essentially that essay's argument compressed into operational form. Its "agents fake done / demand independent proof" cluster reinforces the verification-first position of [[Agentic Verification]], though the playbook goes further into org-level proof (events, auditability, video-driven development). And its human-factors patterns — attention as budget, coaches and players, earn the interruption — complicate [[Five Mistakes People Make with Agentic]], which diagnoses individual failure modes where the playbook designs the supervision structures that prevent them.
