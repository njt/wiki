# The Short Age of Dragon Riders (Atilloy)

A theoretical physicist's November 2026 essay declaring that models in a proper harness now beat most physicists at technical work, mourning the already-dead manual craft of his field, refusing the "process was the point" consolation, and prescribing "riding the dragon": delegate execution, move up in abstraction, contribute taste and direction while the window lasts.

---

The essay is a field report from inside the frontier. Since ~August 2026, the author argues, public models execute the bulk of what a junior theoretical physicist did in a PhD — lengthy calculations, proofs, simulation-verified iteration — with minimal input. Models still lack research taste (good questions, sparks to escape dead ends), but RLVR has removed human data as a bottleneck, internal models are a generation ahead, and compute keeps scaling; so progress is "not going to stop" short of kinetic strikes or a Butlerian Jihad. His prescription for the transient: grieve fast, refuse "vegan science," and ride.

## Key quotes

> "Those who deny this progress have just not tried hard enough (with the latest models), or are blinded by their ego."

The same gatekeeping line appears in every capability essay; here it comes from someone whose identity was built on exactly the skill being automated, which gives it more weight than most.

> "Seeing the process as an end in itself, rather than a means to an end, is profoundly self-serving, and sadly typical of academia."

His sharpest blow is against the 25 Fields medalists who pivoted to "the friends we made along the way." He calls it sore-loser talk and defends results as the point of research — an unusually blunt rejection of the dignity-of-craft retreat.

> "Even if the models do not make any progress, which as I argued is highly unlikely, this is *gone*. Spending too much time on what is gone will soon be unprofessional, like bringing a coloring book to work."

The most honest move in the essay: the manual craft is dead independent of further model progress. No conditional hedging.

> "I suspect the value of good dragon riders will increase exponentially until it collapses dramatically... it is plausible that nothing biological will be able to give valuable insights to the silicon. But we live now, not in three years."

The rider-value bubble is the essay's real thesis: dragon-riding is not a steady-state program but a transient one, with a known terminal condition he cannot name.

## Themes

#concept — riding the dragon: human taste and direction atop machine execution, explicitly analogized to abandoning assembly for compilers
#person — the author as self-declared "techno-optimist absolutist" who takes existential risk seriously yet keeps accelerating
#comparison — "vegan science" (Duminil-Copin's abstinence-as-athlete position) versus dragon-riding
#concept — the transient: a short age between capability threshold and ASI, where human contribution is still valuable but visibly expiring

## Analysis

The essay's strength is its refusal of both available consolations: neither denial (the model can't do real physics) nor reframing (the process was the point). It concedes the territory and then argues about what to do on it. That puts it in a small genre of first-person capability reports from domain elites, and its evidence — "the impossible being done several times" on his own problems since May — is the kind of anecdote that is unfalsifiable but hard to dismiss.

Its weakness is what it can't deliver: the essay admits it cannot say what the right balance of prompting versus pondering is, or what the steady state looks like, or how long the transient is. "Push to the edge of psychosis" is offered as method, which is more confession than recommendation. And the collapse-of-rider-value bubble, while bracing, is asserted rather than argued — a forecast wearing the costume of an observation.

Where it genuinely contributes to the wiki's conversation is in pushing the taste-and-direction argument to its limit: if [[Understand to Participate]] says understanding is the price of staying a collaborator, this essay says understanding at lower levels should be deliberately *abandoned* — you stop reading the assembly. That is a direct, useful complication of the "understand to participate" school, and it sharpens the question every rider-facing source dodges: comprehension at which level, for how long, and with what exit plan.

## Relation to the wiki

This source strengthens [[Understand to Participate]]'s core claim that understanding is the price of staying a collaborator — while directly complicating it: Atilloy argues the understanding that matters has moved up a level, and that clinging to lower-level comprehension is now "like bringing a coloring book to work." It gives [[Building When It Feels Like There's Nothing Left to Build]] a harder-edged sibling answer: where Huyen points to long-tail problems and local preference as durable human territory, Atilloy points at research taste and problem selection, with an expiry date attached. It nuances [[The Joy and Power of Understanding]] by accepting the struggle-is-necessary premise and then declaring the specific struggles of physics practice already dead, joy intact only on the far side of the dragon. And its "edge of psychosis" schedule, collapsing rider value, and lost sleep give first-person texture to the exhaustion and surrender dynamics [[Cognitive Surrender (knlb)]] catalogs from the coding side.
