# Goeckerman Regimen

A nearly century-old treatment for moderate to severe psoriasis combining UVB phototherapy with crude coal tar. First published in 1925, it achieves 100% PASI75 (75% improvement) at 3 months -- better than modern biologics at 67-68%. Average remission lasts 9.5 months to over a year. Safe for elderly, children, and immunosuppressed patients.

---

## Key Quotes

> "Goeckerman therapy is extremely effective and relatively safe for the treatment of psoriasis and for improving a patient's quality of life."

> "100% of patients on Goeckerman therapy achieved PASI75 by 3 months."

> "Clinical studies examining patients on CCT for an extended period of time have not shown an increased risk of skin cancer."

## Key Themes

The core mechanism is synergistic: coal tar is a photosensitizer, so combining it with UVB light produces a stronger response than either alone. The protocol is straightforward -- daily UVB exposure with tar application at increasing concentrations (2% to 5-10%), occluded for 4+ hours, assessed daily.

The timeline is clear: decreased scaling after 10 days, plaque flattening after 20 treatments, complete clearance after 30. Typical course is 18-24 days for 90% clearing.

## Critical Analysis

The remarkable thing about this paper is the implied critique of modern medicine: a 100-year-old treatment outperforms expensive biologics with a better safety profile and longer remission, yet it's underutilized. The reasons are presumably economic (biologics are patentable, coal tar is not) and logistical (Goeckerman requires daily in-office visits for 3-4 weeks).

The safety profile comparison is stark. Biologics carry risks of cancer and serious infections. Goeckerman's worst common side effects are folliculitis and phototoxic reactions. For treatment-resistant cases or patients avoiding systemic medication, this seems like it should be offered more often.

The eczema adaptation (lower tar concentrations, max 5% CCT) extends the protocol's utility beyond its original indication.
