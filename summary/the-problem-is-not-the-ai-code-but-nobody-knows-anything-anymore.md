A short brain-note by Simon Späti arguing that the real damage from agentic coding is not the quality of AI-written code but the loss of human understanding: nobody on a team knows the architecture or the intent behind decisions anymore, and everyone just asks Claude.

He opens by conceding AI writes "probably average code" — if your codebase was below average, it may even improve it. The problem is what's left over: no plan, no architecture knowledge, no intent. A viral-style quote from an engineer at a big company describes the endpoint: specs, code, tests, PRDs and tickets all generated by Claude Code, 12–13 hour days pressing enter, nobody reading anything, nobody resolving bugs, nobody thinking.

Two counterpoints are entertained: data engineers who "grew up pre-AI" had to know everything about the business, so AI merely removes friction for them — but for people starting today, prompting in a new field, that knowledge simply never accrues. And a good non-coding PM could now build anything, but without fundamentals you risk choosing the wrong language or mental model from the start.

His conclusion: hand-writing code might be dead, but taste, intent and architecture are "killer features," and the final boss is still maintainability — the easier it is to generate a quick pipeline or dashboard, the more there is to maintain, and if nobody knows anything, that gets really hard.
