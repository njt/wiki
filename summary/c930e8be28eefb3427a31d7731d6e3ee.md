A vendor webinar transcript from City Access Technologies pitching iVendNext's AI Foresight forecasting module. The diagnosis: retailers lose $1.73T a year not because they can't see their inventory — they all have dashboards — but because visibility never converts into timely action. The speaker names four gaps between seeing and acting: no alert, no owner, no recommended next step, no follow-through ("the task quietly dies at the end of the shift").

The pitch: a nightly classification loop (fast/slow/non-moving/critical, FSN analysis) feeding a "decision queue" that narrows ~2,500 items to only those needing action, reorder recommendations with lead times and safety-stock factors from ARIMA-style ML, and auto-drafted purchase orders left in draft mode for human approval. A second AI Gateway module runs affinity analysis to bundle slow movers into promotions rather than relying purely on markdowns. Claims: at least 10–15% reduction in inventory holding cost, meaningful when ~80% of a retailer's capital sits in stock; Deloitte figures on rising AI supply-chain adoption.

The presenter is refreshingly blunt about the tech ("it is not a crystal ball... it is mathematical"), and "a number on the screen is not a decision" is a genuinely sharp framing. But the talk asserts numbers without sources, glosses over data readiness and alert fatigue, and never explains why a human approval queue won't reproduce the exact follow-through failure the diagnosis names.
