A Grist webinar transcript in which Olga Tataranova, co-founder of Epoch8, explains why her clients' LLM deployments failed until they stopped dumping documents into chat and started handing agents an expert-authored domain model. The method is Alexey Mahotkin's "minimal modeling": any domain is described with three concepts — anchors (the nouns), attributes, and links — stored in Grist as exactly three tables, each with a description, a data example, and a sample query the agent can learn from.

Two use cases fall out of the same notation: feeding the model to an LLM to extract structured entities from documents, and dropping the whole Grist document (it is just SQLite, or post-MCP the Grist MCP) into Claude as context with the instruction to explore and craft queries. In their head-to-head, chunk-dumping into ChatGPT missed respondents and facts; Vedana crafted a Cypher query against Memgraph and returned the exact, sourced value.

Crucially, the model cannot be auto-generated — "Claude misses the point, misses the links, misses domain logic" — so experts must build it, but only once, since domains are stable and experts then self-serve by adding columns. Grist is the editorial backend, not the production query store; an ETL pushes to Memgraph because Grist hits performance walls at hundreds of thousands of rows.
