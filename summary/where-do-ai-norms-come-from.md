Charity Majors kicks off a planned blog dialogue with research psychologist Dr Cat Hicks about norms, ethics, and learning in the AI era — prompted by a reader question following her "Confessions of an Unrepentant Slop Snob" post and Honeycomb's published AI norms and values docs: how should companies that feel the friction but haven't written shared norms yet get started?

The meat is short and practical: her answer to "where do you start?" is **start by listening**. She put opt-in, open-door discussion hours (capped at ten people) on the calendar and asked wide-open questions like "What AI-related thing has annoyed you lately?" Themes emerged early; she followed those threads in later sessions. The listening sessions doubled as a pressure valve — by the time Honeycomb released its AI norms and values documents internally, "the heat had simmered down quite a bit."

The rest is framing. Majors draws a parallel between Hicks' *The Psychology of Software Teams* and her own second edition of *Observability Engineering*: radically different books making the same case about what feeds greatness in people and teams, responsibility to each other, and governance of sociotechnical systems. She names the unsettled mood of the moment — managers expecting 10x while teams deliver 2x, and "the only honest answer is that no one really knows yet" whether expectations or output should adjust. Hicks' response is to come at her own newsletter (fightforthehuman), making this the opening move of a distributed two-blog conversation.
