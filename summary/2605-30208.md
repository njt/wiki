Meta deployed RADAR (Risk Aware Diff Auto Review), an end-to-end funnel that automates the review and landing of low-to-medium-risk diffs while routing higher-risk changes to humans. It was built in response to a squeeze the paper quantifies starkly: significant lines of code per human-landed diff grew 105.9% year over year and diffs per developer rose 51%, with 80%+ of that growth attributed to agentic AI — while the share of diffs reviewed within 24 hours declined. Human review capacity is the bottleneck the agents created.

The funnel is layered, not a single model. Every diff is classified by authorship (human or bot) and by automation source, and each source gets a distinct eligibility path: deterministic codemods get Blanket AutoAccept (the transformation is vetted at the codemod level, so no per-diff review); AI-generated codemods and RACER runbooks go through the ACE pipeline (static safety checks → Diff Risk Score threshold → LLM-based Automated Code Review); human-authored diffs go through RADAR Verification (ship with deferred review) and optionally RADAR Approval (no review at all). The Diff Risk Score (DRS), a production-incident-predicting ML model already powering ~20 risk features at Meta, is the calibration knob: thresholds are percentiles (P5 = only the safest 5% qualify), set per organization, per runbook, and per source type. RACER runbooks are individually evaluated on 60-day risk history (zero PIs, low revert rate, minimum volume), daily landing caps, and a denylist — two runbooks with identical transformations are treated differently if one has a revert history.

Results across 535K+ RADAR-reviewed and 331K+ landed diffs (peaking at 25K/day): approve rate of 60.31% after relaxing the threshold from P25 to P50, a revert rate 1/3 of non-RADAR diffs, a production-incident rate 1/50 of non-RADAR diffs, median time-to-close reduced by over 330%, and median review wall time down 35% versus human-reviewed diffs. The authors are candid about method limits: no random assignment, observational and difference-in-differences designs only, no full model specification, and no diff-level data released. All PIs in the cohort were manually reviewed by domain experts — and notably, none were detected by a human reviewer.

The paper's deepest argument is an inversion: prior risk models surfaced risk to reviewers; RADAR uses risk scores to identify diffs that need *no* reviewer, automating the entire review-to-landing pipeline for the low-risk tail. It also names the trade-off the automation literature mostly ignores — review is knowledge transfer, and every automated review removes a channel for it. The authors report the trade-off is currently favorable but flag it may shift as coverage expands.
