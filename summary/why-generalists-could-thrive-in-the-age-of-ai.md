A 2m19s YouTube clip from Isaac Levin's channel, transcribed from video. Two speakers open with a complaint about the over-stuffed modern day — 16–18 waking hours spent context-switching, attention spans shrinking — which Speaker B historicises: there were eras of abundant free time and eras of brutal industrial-revolution hours, and today's pressure is uneven (they single out competitive Silicon Valley parenting and its enrichment-class arms race).

The pivot arrives via David Epstein's book *Range*: it "debunks this idea that for you to be really good at something, you have to do just that thing forever." Speaker B offers their own life as the counter-example to avoid — programming since age nine — and cites the book's finding that many top performers changed careers late (Charles Darwin was headed for the priesthood). Breadth, the argument goes, is what helps.

The closing line is the thesis: "maybe in this age of AI being a generalist might be even more important to be able to connect the dots and not be just so focused on one thing." When generation is cheap, the valuable act is seeing which dots connect.
