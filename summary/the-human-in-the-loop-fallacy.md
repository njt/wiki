# The "Human-in-the-Loop" Fallacy

Choudary argues that "human-in-the-loop" — the most repeated phrase in enterprise AI adoption — is an organizational trap. It assigns people to the shrinking residue of what machines can't yet do, and the roles it creates are self-liquidating: every correction is training data, every caught exception teaches the system to handle it. Automation research (Bainbridge's "irony of automation") predicts complacent, skill-atrophying supervisors of increasingly reliable systems.

The alternative is to read the transformation forwards rather than backwards. AI removes execution constraints; removing constraints expands the range of choices an organization can make. Choudary's examples: The Mandalorian's LED volumes moved post-production decisions onto the set; BBC Verify moved journalists upstream from drafting to establishing provenance; IKEA retrained 8,500 call-centre workers into remote interior design that generated €1.3B; Garfield Law built a new market for £2 debt claims; John Deere internalized AI capabilities, reorganized around production systems, and created its first CTO.

His prescriptions: map fallen constraints, not automatable tasks; fund frontier cases, not just efficiency cases; continuously assess human capabilities against the AI capability curve; embed governance into the work (Morgan Stanley's daily regression suite of "golden questions") rather than bolting review on at the end. A reorganization that only adds titles is task reallocation — firms that fail to internalize the learning cycle surrender the new control points to tool providers while turning their workforce into trainers of someone else's advantage.
