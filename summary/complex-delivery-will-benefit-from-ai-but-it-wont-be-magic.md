An essay on AI in complex programme delivery, focused on reporting rather than code generation. The author's central observation comes from watching AI agents work: the agents always knew their current status because the work and the record of the work were the same thing. If every worker, human or AI, leaves a structured trace as part of doing the work, the status pack stops being a document assembled over two days from emails and memory, and becomes a view over the work itself.

The real problem, the author argues, is not the PowerPoint. It is that programme information lives in too many places, is captured inconsistently, and is not trusted — so people ask a person instead of a system. AI drafting status reports does not fix this; the discipline problem remains. What AI can do is reduce the friction of maintaining the system of work (turning normal conversation into structured updates), apply rubrics consistently where humans apply them inconsistently, and improve data quality at the point of entry rather than only at the point of reporting.

The piece closes with six principles the author has distilled from practice — establish the source of truth first, make updating it part of the work, use what native systems already give you, report from the system not an extract, use AI where synthesis is required, and use AI at the point of entry — and a redefinition of the PMO role from producing the truth to governing it. AI can make a programme dramatically more observable; it cannot make leaders pay attention.
