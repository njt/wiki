# AI Brains Landscape

A working, vendor-audited map of "AI brain" tools — the products and infrastructure that keep memory for people, companies, agents, and documents — sorted by job-to-be-done rather than by marketing category. The page is a public snapshot, not procurement advice: 53 entries across four tiers (buyer-facing products, memory infrastructure, build substrates, personal/emerging tools), re-verified 18–19 October 2026 against vendor sites, docs, repos and package registries, with 251 source URLs probed and open row-by-row citations.

The framing device is use cases first: pick what you need AI to do — keep a personal brain, remember people, understand company knowledge, answer from documents, run workflows, or give employees an approved assistant where they already work — then use the comparison table for the details. A recurring theme in the reading guidance is MCP as a differentiator: 34 of the 53 entries ship a first-party MCP server, each claim independently re-checked against npm, PyPI and vendor docs, with package-identity impostors rejected.

The page's central thesis: the useful split is **shared company memory** ("what does the organization know?") versus **agent-specific adaptive memory** ("what has this AI worker learned from doing the job?"), with **transactive memory** — who or what knows this, and where the authoritative version lives — as the connective tissue. The serious product will need both, plus governance; otherwise you get "a smart search box that never learns from work, or a learning agent with no trustworthy company context." Governance — permissions, provenance, audit logs, retention, deletion, memory-poisoning defenses — is positioned as something to verify before rollout, not after.
