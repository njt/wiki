Briana May's HUMAN.md is a working manifesto — "notes-to-self, mostly" — on what humans still bring to creative work now that models are very good and improving daily. Written in the voice of a practitioner's personal README rather than a thinkpiece, it names ten principles for staying the author of your own output: break rules on purpose (with a reason), find inspiration in strange places, advocate for the human at the table, design for emotional resonance over correctness, seek delight, experiment and ship rather than opine, own the output fully, work *with* the model in the loop rather than as a spectator, play both with and without purpose, and be both a builder and an ideas person.

The strongest thread is accountability: "If the AI output is bad, the AI did not fail you. *You* failed it." AI is "not the great equalizer, it is the great amplifier" — it rewards people who showed up sharp and exposes people who didn't. Taste, she argues, is too small a word for what humans contribute; beneath it is an engine of judgment, advocacy, and deliberate rule-breaking that the model can't have.

The piece ends honestly: "This file will be wrong in six months. That is fine. I'd rather be specifically wrong than vaguely correct."
