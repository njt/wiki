Suzan Bond's Constellary Org Debt Framework extends the technical-debt analogy to organisations: "organizational debt is the workarounds and unresolved gaps that accumulate as a company changes, creating obstacles to its goals." Where tech debt makes software slow and buggy, org debt makes teams unable to collaborate, decisions hard, and the best people leaving.

The article splits the lump category into five types, each with an "org smell" list modelled on code smells: **leadership debt** (capabilities and infrastructure that haven't kept pace — avoided conversations, missing layers, bottlenecks), **relational debt** (eroded trust and psychological safety, information hoarding, "us vs them"), **structural debt** (org charts designed for yesterday's priorities), **systems debt** (duct-tape tools and manual workarounds), and **reality debt** (plans built on best-case assumptions — the most dangerous, because it compounds all the others).

The framework's real contribution is the interaction analysis: named reinforcing cycles (reality → leadership → relational; structural enables systems; systems worsens reality) and three predictable combination patterns — startups scaling (reality + structural + leadership), rapid growth (systems + structural + reality), and post-merger where all five arrive at once. The prescription resists the temptation of tangible fixes: redrawn org charts are "new structures for old behaviors" unless leadership changes first, with reality debt tackled alongside it.
