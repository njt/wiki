A vendor essay from Stack Overflow arguing that AI has solved retrieval but not the harder problem: when three sources disagree about a consequential business question, someone must still decide what the organization can stand behind. The piece names the missing artifact "decision-grade knowledge" — answers that carry provenance, applicability (which version/region/config), permissions, visible contradictions, and a named person who can resolve conflict.

The core argument: fast retrieval makes the gap worse, not better. An answer that sounds complete but leaves the human doing the last, most consequential verification step is "where many AI experiences lose people." The remedy is not more model quality but a knowledge layer where expert corrections persist, stay connected to what they correct, and start the next team's investigation from work already done. Crucially, experts should review only uncertain, conflicting, or high-traffic answers — "we do not want those people reviewing every response."

The second half is the product pitch: the general availability of Stack Internal, which brings org sources into a shared validated layer served through chat, API, and MCP — the same knowledge basis for a human in chat, an app via API, or an agent via MCP. It closes with a roadmap toward capturing one-to-one conversations (human or agent) as reusable knowledge before the session ends, so "a hard-won answer does not disappear with the meeting."
