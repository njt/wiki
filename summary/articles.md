The article corpus behind the Software Engineering Practice Atlas: 5,172 crawled
engineering reports — blog posts, incident reports, and talks from named sources
like Hacker News, The Pragmatic Engineer, Cloudflare Blog, ByteByteGo, Martin
Fowler, and Simon Willison — exposed as a filterable feed.

Each report is classified along three axes. **Outcome** (exactly one per report):
Win (1,620), Migration (130), Lesson (2,734), Outage (566), Postmortem (122).
**Phase** (multi-valued): Process (1,049), Requirements (906), Design (1,700),
Development (520), Testing (575), Releasing (249), Operation (481), Maintenance
(163). **Quality attribute** (multi-valued): Correctness (2,981), Reliability
(1,649), Performance (984), Scalability (763), Security (550), Maintainability
(1,286), Operability (745), Usability (819), Cost (1,204), Compliance (561).

Every entry carries its source, age, reading time, title, a one-line summary,
phase and quality-attribute tags, and a "Relates to" list that wires the article
to the atlas's concept cards — turning a pile of articles into a browsable graph
rather than a list.

The distribution is telling: lessons outnumber every other outcome, and
correctness is the most-tagged attribute. This is the evidence layer beneath the
atlas's synthesized (AI-generated) practice cards — the human-written source
material the cards are distilled from, and the "verify before you rely" escape
hatch.
