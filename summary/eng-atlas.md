The Software Engineering Practice Atlas is a curated collection of software
craft knowledge — practices, principles, patterns, and metrics — gathered into
one navigable map. It draws from books, talks, and blog posts so practitioners
don't have to stumble across them one piece at a time.

The atlas is structured as five "practice maps," each a board of cards:
**Engineering** (1,512 cards covering ten quality attributes across the
software lifecycle), **Engineering Management** (331 cards across nine
responsibility lanes), **Product Management** (414 cards), **Project &
Program Management** (284 cards), and **Research** (2,113 cards across ten
stages from framing a question to stewarding a program). Twenty-five field
guides provide domain-specific lenses — distributed systems, data, ML/AI, web,
mobile, backend, enterprise, cloud infra, and a dozen research subfields — each
walking the same quality attributes but only where the domain genuinely
reshapes them.

Every card states what the practice is, why it matters, how to apply it, and
when *not* to use it. Cards link to related cards, sources, and crawled
engineering-blog articles. The site is honest about its limits: depth varies,
most content is AI-generated and not yet human copy-edited, and some domains
are less complete than others. It is best treated as a map to explore, not as
gospel.
