Wes McKinney, creator of Pandas and co-creator of Apache Arrow, in conversation with Dan Beach on the Data Engineering Central Podcast. The episode covers Pandas' origin story, Arrow's slow adoption curve, the economics of open source trust, and the limits of AI for foundational data infrastructure.

Pandas was born in 2007–2008 from frustration with Excel and proprietary MATLAB at a quant hedge fund. Wes built "Proto Pandas" as internal research tooling, open-sourced it in 2010, and watched Python win data science by being approachable — a book and good documentation lowered the barrier. He notes Pandas was "internally a bit of a mess" but succeeded because it prioritized human ergonomics over architectural purity.

Apache Arrow, started at Cloudera in 2016, is described as category-defining technology that people initially thought impossible to standardize. Its value compounds with adoption, and today it underpins DuckDB, Polars, Daft, and the modern data stack. Wes quotes DuckDB co-creator Hannes Mühleisen on AI's inability to replicate such systems: "injection molded plastic toys versus building a fine Swiss watch."

The conversation touches persistent data problems — moving data between systems, format conversion, the "last mile" — and the shift from the data-engineering boom toward AI tooling. Wes is cautiously optimistic about AI: it rewards people with agency, taste, and judgment, but risks turning those without into "slop cannons." He advises new engineers to study software architecture and data systems, not just coding.
