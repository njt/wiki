## Referenced article: "Welcome to the American Winter"

Author: Robert F. Worth (contributing writer at The Atlantic, former New York Times bureau chief)
Published: January 25, 2026 (updated January 29, 2026)

Long-form on-the-ground reportage from Minneapolis in early 2026, documenting a decentralized, community-organized resistance against a massive federal immigration enforcement surge ("Operation Metro Surge").

Key details from the article:
- Six-car ICE convoy swarmed by protesters using coordinated tactics: identical whistles, chants, networked via Signal
- Described as "meticulous urban choreography of civic protest" built on networks from the 2020 George Floyd uprising
- Profiles ordinary citizens (teachers, retirees, driving instructors, clergy) who underwent legal-observer training and role-playing exercises to confront armed federal agents nonviolently
- Draws parallels to the 2011 Arab Spring (Tahrir Square): leaderless movement organized by neighborhood via encrypted chat
- Two fatal shootings by federal agents: Renee Good (shot in the face, January 7) and Alex Pretti (ICU nurse, shot at point-blank range, January 24)
- 65,000 Minnesotans trained as of January 2026; clergy, churches, suburban residents joined in large numbers
- Economic crisis: immigrant-owned businesses saw sales drop up to 80%, schools went virtual

Outcome: By February 13, 2026, the federal "Operation Metro Surge" was formally ended — widely seen as a victory for the resistance.
