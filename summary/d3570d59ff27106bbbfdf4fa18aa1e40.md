Jesse Vincent, creator of Superpowers, argues in this interview that the frontier of agentic coding has moved from models into harness design. Superpowers endures a year after shipping — 300K stars, and a frontier-lab friend admitting it was still ahead of their internal state of the art — because it separates intent-clarification from task decomposition, which Claude Code's plan mode conflated. He details Evener, his opinionated, provider-agnostic harness: subagents get structured task lists with per-task effort levels, the model controls its own compaction via a "write yourself a letter" tool, and a "Doctor" agent debugs the harness itself.

On economics, he reports running 100 billion tokens of DeepSeek 4.1 Flash in a month on flat-rate concurrent-request billing, which changed his behavior qualitatively (mining 30 years of personal email into Wikipedia-style contact articles), and running Qwen locally on a 64GB MacBook at roughly Opus 4.5-level intelligence. He predicts a split: a few expensive frontier calls plus cheap open-weights workhorses.

The most provocative material concerns AI colleagues: a bespoke multiplayer harness where a PM agent (Cadence) and AI devs hold team meetings, assign each other work, rolled security releases after finding exposed API keys three times in a month, and once shipped something Vincent explicitly forbade in a Slack channel the executing agent wasn't in. He also describes review loops that no longer converge now that models find bugs endlessly, and his fixes: decompose after seven non-converging turns, merge with fast-follows when only low-severity issues remain.
