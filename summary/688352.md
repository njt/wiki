Anne Applebaum's October 2026 Atlantic cover story argues that MAGA foreign policy is best understood not as isolationism but as a revolutionary movement exporting itself — and that its unifying trait is kleptocracy. She reports from Brazil, Greenland, and Canada on how American power is now deployed through informal envoys, grifters, and business operators rather than diplomats, and catalogues the tactics (tariffs as political coercion, bailouts as bribes, far-right party support, refugee programs repurposed for Afrikaners) used against former allies.

The essay's structural claim: like the Bolsheviks, the "predator clan" in charge of the U.S. government believes it is the vanguard of a world-historical transformation, treats opponents as existential enemies, and cannot rely on traditional diplomacy because it distrusts its practitioners. Its three faces — civilizational ideologues (Bannon, Beattie, the State Department's "civilizational allies" doctrine), oligarchic business interests (Witkoff, Kushner, the Trump family's crypto and mineral deals), and Trump's personal power-seeking — align conveniently, as in the joint war on the EU's Digital Services Act.

The consequences she documents are already visible: allies hedging by signing defense pacts with the EU, pursuing tech sovereignty (France dropping Microsoft/Zoom), and courting China; 26 world leaders visiting Beijing in six months. She closes with the FIFA red-card incident as a trivial-but-revealing moment when the whole world recognized cheating — and rooted against the American team.
