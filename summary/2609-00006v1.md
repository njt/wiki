A source-code anatomy of eleven production coding-agent harnesses — Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, Mistral Vibe, OpenHands, Aider, Mini-SWE-Agent, Hermes, Pi, OpenCode, and OpenClaw, plus Omnigent (Databricks) as a contrast meta-harness — pinned to July 2026 releases. It defines the harness, maps seven canonical subsystems (agent loop, LLM integration, tools & actions, memory & context, safety & permissions, orchestration, extensibility) with minimal and maximal implementations of each, and yields 13 cross-cutting observations and 29 recurring design patterns.

The paper's two headline empirical absences, both surviving a threefold corpus expansion: across roughly 4M lines of Python/TypeScript/Rust, no agent runtime imports a general-purpose agentic framework (LangChain, LangGraph, AutoGen, etc. — Gemini CLI uses neither of Google's own), and none retrieves code with vector-embedding RAG. Instead: hand-rolled async loops, ripgrep/tree-sitter/glob, and auto-discovered Markdown context files. SKILL.md skills beat MCP in adoption (9/11 vs 8/11); ACP ships in six systems and acquired a third role, harness hosting.

Because eight systems were re-pinned from an April 2026 edition, the study carries a controlled ninety-day longitudinal diff: convergence became traceable imitation (Codex adopted Claude Code's hook vocabulary verbatim and ships an importer for its sessions; OpenHands reads Claude Code's plugin format), patterns diffused down the corpus, and behavioral policy migrated from prompt prose to configuration. The closing thesis: the harness completed a platform turn in H1 2026 — harnesses became importable SDKs, framework vendors shipped harnesses, marketplaces and switching-cost tooling appeared, and the first meta-harness orchestrates eleven vendor harnesses behind one API. Closes with 18 design recommendations and a 90-line minimum-viable-harness scaffold implementing ten of them.
