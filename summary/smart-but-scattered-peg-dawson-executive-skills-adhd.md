# ytx: Smart But Scattered — Dr. Peg Dawson on Executive Skills in Children with ADHD

Gist containing a summary and full transcript of Dr. Peg Dawson's presentation to the Radnor Township School District on executive skills, drawing on decades of clinical work, her *Smart but Scattered* book series (co-authored with Dr. Richard Guare), and her personal experience raising a son diagnosed with an attention disorder at age 14.

## Summary — Key Points

- **11 executive skills in two tiers:** The first six are foundational (response inhibition, working memory, emotional control, flexibility, sustained attention, task initiation); the last five are higher-order skills built on that foundation (planning/prioritization, organization, time management, goal-directed persistence, metacognition).
- **The DSM-ADHD mismatch:** The APA's diagnostic criteria (inattention and hyperactivity) fail to capture the real struggles — organization, time management, planning, and task initiation.
- **Brain development timeline:** The prefrontal cortex isn't fully mature until roughly age 25. MRI scans show frontal lobes still largely immature at age 13 — precisely when middle schools demand complex scheduling and long-term projects.
- **Reframing "lazy" as skill deficit:** Character labels like "lazy" offer no intervention path. Executive skill language normalizes struggle as improvable capacity. "What's the intervention for lazy? … We have interventions for task initiation, sustained attention."
- **Parents as surrogate frontal lobes:** Adults must modify environments and tasks while gradually transferring responsibility to the child. Support needs shift within a single school year.
- **Practical strategies:** Teaching "wait" and "stop" for response inhibition; stated start times paired with action to build task initiation via brain association (Pavlovian); visual checklists with photos for organization; study plan templates integrating time estimation, planning, and mental rehearsal.
- **Praise process, not traits:** "Calling a kid smart is probably one of the worst things you can do." Specific praise for effort, persistence, and strategy-building. 3:1 praise-to-corrective-feedback ratio.
- **Incentives as practice enablers:** Rewards are not bribes but tools to get reluctant kids the practice repetitions they need for skill-building.
- **Goal-directed persistence as the ultimate prize:** Often doesn't emerge until late high school or beyond. Dawson's own sons appeared unmotivated at 14 but threw themselves into their chosen careers once they found their passion.
- **Debriefing successes:** Asking "what did you do differently?" when things go well reinforces effective strategies and builds metacognition. Most parents only debrief failures.
