Glyph Lefkowitz (creator of Twisted and `Deferred`) argues against the resignation many programmers feel about AI-generated code — the "it's just functional work, so it doesn't matter" excuse. Against the special status other creative industries have claimed in resisting AI, he insists that **programming is Art**, and that this is exactly why the same resistance applies.

The argument leans on John Berger's "mystification": we wrongly divide creative work into transcendent art and mundane function, when the transcendent/mundane line cuts through every medium — novelist vs journalist vs copywriter, fine art vs commissioned portrait. Most code is "just copy-writing", but so is most writing, most visual art, and most live music.

His own invention of `Deferred` is the worked example: it was an *aesthetic* reaction to the tedium of callback/errback RPC plumbing, and it was influential precisely because of that aesthetic focus — a "folk epic poetry" tradition of artisans iterating on each other's work down to `async`/`await`.

The payoff is about practice and pipeline: if AI erases all the mundane work (the copywriting, the WordPress themes), it removes the on-the-job training ground where craft develops. Each mundane project has a ~0.1% chance of greatness; automating *all* of them takes the aggregate from "sometimes" to "never". Using AI to destroy creative decision-making, he concludes, is "slop is slop, no matter the medium" — worth resisting in software exactly as in any creative medium.
