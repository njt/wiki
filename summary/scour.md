Scour is a personalized content feed built by solo developer Evan Schwartz. It
ingests posts from Hacker News, Reddit, arXiv, Substack, RSS feeds, and
thousands of blogs and newsletters, then uses semantic matching against
free-form interest descriptions to surface relevant articles.

The key differentiator is relevance-based ranking: a post with one upvote can
outrank one with a thousand if it better matches the user's interests. This
deliberately favors hidden gems — small personal blogs, niche sites — over raw
popularity. Users can curate which sources to search or search all of them.

The site is entirely keyboard-navigable with vim-style shortcuts for browsing,
rating, saving, and discovering new interests and feeds. It also offers a weekly
email digest and public feed sharing.

User testimonials describe it as a "serendipity machine" and a replacement for
doom scrolling, comparing it favorably to manually maintaining hundreds of RSS
feeds or relying on subreddit curation. As of mid-2026, it scoured over 800,000
posts per month from 28,000+ community-added sources.
