Michael Lopp (Rands) traces the five writers who directly shaped his own voice, in chronological order of when he encountered them. It is a personal genealogy of a writing style, running from 1988 to 2002 — the whole arc of his pre-blogging reading life.

The list: Robert Fulghum's *All I Really Need to Know I Learned in Kindergarten* taught him to name unnamed parts of the human condition and to stay hopeful. Robert B. Parker's Spenser novels taught him that 80% of a book can be dialogue — "gritty, intelligent dialogue" where villains are unmasked because they cannot keep up the banter. Robert X. Cringely's *Accidental Empires* and his InfoWorld gossip column showed that tech stories, "real or not," can both educate and become legend — he was "not a historian, but an explainer." Douglas Coupland's *Microserfs* reflected his early-90s tech life so precisely that fiction read like nonfiction and made him feel less of an outsider. And Michael Sippey's blog *Stating the Obvious* (1995) gave him permission to start his own blog, *The Bitsifter Digest*, in 1996 — bad writing at first, which he stopped in 1999; Sippey never stopped.

The payoff paragraph attributes each ingredient of the Rands voice to a source: conversational tone and snark from Parker, hope from Fulghum, storytelling from Coupland, discipline-required obviousness from Sippey, and legend-building from Cringely. He closes with a nod to Simon Travaglia's still-updating BOFH journals on The Register.
