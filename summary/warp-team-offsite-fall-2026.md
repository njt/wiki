A short company-blog post from Warp's fall 2026 offsite in Savannah, announcing that **Warp Factories** — their open infrastructure for teams running "software factories" — is moving from Early Access to general availability. The stated mandate from CEO Zach Lloyd: reliability, latency, cost.

The headline framing is strategic rather than technical: Warp says it has "largely shifted from a product company into an infrastructure company," building the substrate for "everything an agent can do, including multi-agent orchestration and computer-use verification." The one hard number: driving their own team's cost per PR down 70% in the past month using Warp Factories as their agent infrastructure, with those learnings being baked into the product. The rest is offsite color (kayaking) and a hiring CTA.

The post is thin — more press release than engineering write-up — but it is a data point on the software-factory market: a vendor openly repositioning from developer tool to agent infrastructure provider, with cost-per-PR as the metric of merit.
