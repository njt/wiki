This peer-reviewed study from SUNY College of Optometry tested whether the
widely promoted "20-20-20 rule" — take a 20-second break every 20 minutes to
look at something 20 feet away — actually reduces digital eye strain. The
authors note there was little prior evidence supporting the rule despite its
popularity.

Thirty young participants completed a 40-minute cognitively demanding reading
task on a tablet under four conditions: 20-second breaks every 5, 10, 20, or
40 minutes (the last being effectively no break). Pre- and post-task symptom
questionnaires, reading speed, and task accuracy were measured.

Post-task symptoms increased significantly across all conditions. However,
scheduled breaks had **no significant effect** on reported symptoms (P = .70),
reading speed (P = .93), or task accuracy (P = .55).

The authors conclude their results "do not support the proposal of using
20-second scheduled breaks as a therapeutic intervention for digital eye
strain." Note the study tested only 20-second breaks at varying intervals on a
single reading task — it does not rule out that longer breaks, different
activities, or real-world use patterns might help.
