A ytx gist transcription (structured summary + full transcript) of Tricia Broderick's day-one keynote "Building Resilience". Broderick is a leadership advisor with 25+ years in software, co-author with Diana Larsen of *Lead Without Blame*, and founder of Ignite Insight + Innovation; the venue is inferred as Craft 2025 from the same-day Kent Beck keynote and the ytx-gist series this wiki has already ingested. Her thesis is negative first: there is no magical checklist for resilience — the ability, personal and collective, to handle it "when crap goes down," and crap is the norm ("I didn't even know an inland hurricane was possible"). Anyone selling steps is "full of it." What exist are patterns, offered as four pieces of the puzzle — compassion, confidence, courage, complexity — explicitly not the complete picture.

Compassion attacks fake teams. The spectrum runs individuals → groups (cooperation — a relay race where "if that second person falls down, no one blames the anchor," and you can still earn a bonus for your leg while the team loses) → teams (collaboration, where better ideas and co-intelligence live), and organizations are "not only organized for groups, but they're optimized for group behavior" precisely when knowledge work requires discovering what value even is. Getting to teams requires leaders who stop being the hub — a skill nobody taught them (strategy and delegation, yes; facilitation and mentoring, no) — and COVID showed how fast high-performing teams revert to "just give me my part." Confidence comes from the Queen of Hearts card trick, performed live on an audience volunteer: with a power dynamic, a leader can always steer you to the answer in their head; "manipulation is not leadership and it's not trust"; the fix is intentional teaching situations, because "you will stay the expert if you continue to act like you're the only expert." Courage cannot be commanded — "have courage" works exactly as well as "calm down" (never) — so her lever, as someone "in a position of privilege and power," is pricing her silence: "What is my silence saying when I choose not to speak up?"

Complexity is where learning becomes daily work rather than a quarterly side project, illustrated by her 18-month handstand journey and its five inhibitors: baggage (goals set by perceived success — the mentee who became an executive and called to say "I hate my life"), change (non-linear; "conference Tricia" trying to take her department from 18-month releases to "feasible daily deploy" in one move and having to pull back to CI and automation), unknown (Adam Grant's Mount Stupid; she learned to hold a handstand but never learned to fall), time ("six weeks to a handstand. They lie."), and failure (gaming metrics — slowing her own Camtasia video to fake the hold — isn't learning). She hit the 10-second handstand exactly once, at 18 months to the day, and never again — and that is the point: "there is no done," "it was never about the check mark," and "how you build resilience is by being resilient."

The gist's digest is unusually serviceable and unusually honest: a toolkit of takeables (the relay-race test, the hub audit, the Queen of Hearts self-check, the silence check, exploratory steps whose only purpose is to unlock unknowns, change pacing, recording daily practice, building a community for when "today was horrible") followed by a "left on the table" section that catalogues the gaps — de-hubbing mechanics offstage, the COVID confession unresolved, courage advice that assumes privilege, systemic causes with individual cures, no boundary between resilience and burnout, and a transcript that cuts before the live on-stage handstand attempt resolves.
