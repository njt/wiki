Harth is a browser-based, AI-powered visual workspace for architecture teams. Its pitch: bring your 3D building data in from Revit, Archicad, SketchUp, or IFC, and get every frontier model (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Kimi) plus every image model (Nano Banana Pro, Seedance, Flux, Qwen) on one spatial canvas — no installs, no per-model subscriptions, no version conflicts.

The product claims fall into three groups. First, model access: "every frontier model an architecture team could ever need," switchable without leaving the canvas, with multi-model rendering and visualization ("visualize a 3D space in any photo or graphic style in minutes, not days"). Second, purpose-built agents: "purpose-built agents can handle your complex architectural work," sequenced in parallel to "handle the boilerplate while you stay on the design," plus deep research on new sites that yields "a substantiated report you can trust." Third, workspace features: 3D satellite terrain import, neighboring-building context, comments anchored on the 3D model itself, image-to-3D object conversion, multiplayer editing, and client-facing share links with PDF/image/3D export.

The positioning is explicitly coexistence rather than replacement: "Harth works alongside your CAD, not instead of it. Your CAD stays where the building becomes precise." The FAQ closes with the same reassurance — keep using Revit, ArchiCAD, or Vectorworks; "Harth takes one export and handles everything from there."

As a source this is a marketing landing page: undated, unattributed, and short on specifics about how the agents actually work, what data model they operate on, or what "complex architectural work" they can be trusted with. It is useful as evidence of a pattern — vertical agent workspaces wrapping frontier models for a non-software profession — rather than as a technical account.
