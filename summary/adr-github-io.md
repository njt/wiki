The home page of the GitHub `adr` organization is the canonical landing point for the Architectural Decision Record (ADR) practice. It is short: definitions, an aim statement, media mentions, and a reading list.

The definitions section does the real work. An **Architectural Decision (AD)** is "a justified design choice that addresses a functional or non-functional requirement that is architecturally significant". An **Architecturally Significant Requirement (ASR)** is one with a measurable effect on the architecture and quality of a system. An **ADR** captures a single AD and its rationale — simply put, it lets you understand why a choice was made, along with its trade-offs and consequences. The collection of ADRs in a project is its **decision log**, and the whole apparatus sits under Architectural Knowledge Management (AKM), extendable to "any decision record".

The organization's stated aims: motivate the need for capturing decisions and establish a common vocabulary; strengthen tooling around ADRs in support of agile and iterative processes; and provide pointers to public knowledge.

The background section grounds the practice historically: the site's work builds on *Sustainable Architectural Decisions* by Zdun et al. (including the Y-statement format), Michael Nygard's 2011 blog post *Documenting Architecture Decisions* that popularized the concept, a WICSA 2015 comparison of seven ADR templates, and recent mainstreaming signals — ADRs appear in the Azure Well-Architected Framework (2024) and AWS Prescriptive Guidance, plus a Michael Keeling IEEE Software column (2022) on how ADRs reconciled architecture with agile.
