O'Reilly Radar piece arguing that the overlooked AI disruption is on the buy side of enterprise B2B, not the sell side. While commentary fixates on consumer shopping agents, companies like Maersk already run autonomous procurement: Pactum's agents negotiated freight contracts end-to-end at a 96% agreement rate and secured rates 22% below human negotiators in trials. G2's April 2026 survey shows 51% of B2B buyers now start research with AI chatbots, meaning "all the selling was done by the answer engine" before a seller is ever contacted.

The article examines three commercial pillars built for human buyers and how agents strain each: per-seat licensing (which cannot map to continuously running, parallel-spawning agents — pushing Sierra and Intercom toward outcome-based pricing), relationship-driven sales (agents evaluate only measurable terms during screening, so rapport-building arrives too late), and quarterly-business-review retention (agents compute ROI from usage logs and cross-reference competitor pricing, making churn algorithmic rather than social).

It closes with three adaptations: outcome-based or consumption pricing, machine-readable product surfaces via protocols like the Universal Commerce Protocol and Google's Agent2Agent Agent Cards, and agent-compatible authorization so autonomous workflows can actually complete transactions. Underlying data points: non-human identities outnumber humans 144:1 in enterprises, and Gartner projects AI agents will outnumber human sellers tenfold by 2028.
