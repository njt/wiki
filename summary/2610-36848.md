An arXiv paper (submitted 27 Oct 2026) introducing **Evolving Agent Teams**, a multi-agent framework for escaping the local optima that single coding agents converge to on hard, expert-written optimization problems — the flagship example being GPU kernel optimization against production FlashInfer kernels.

The framework has two components. First, a **central supervisor** that diagnoses each local optimum the team has reached and *restructures* the team of agents in response — not just re-prompting, but changing who is on the team and how they are organized. Second, a **persistent file system** — a learning document, an evolution history, and per-team artifacts — that grounds each restructuring in accumulated experience rather than a fresh context.

Results under a matched token budget consistently beat strong coding-agent baselines. With an extended budget, the system generates a CUDA kernel from scratch reaching 1.2x mean speedup over the production FlashInfer MLA paged decode kernel, winning on 36 of 47 FlashInfer-Bench workloads (0.86–0.99x on the rest). Other wins: KDA prefill on AMD MI350X (3.01x over vLLM) and NVIDIA B200 (3.44x over FlashKDA), MoE expert-offload serving on Apple silicon (30.3 tok/s decode for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next in MLX 4-bit on a 48 GB M4 Pro), and RoboDojo robot manipulation (62.1 on held-out seeds vs 55.3 for the best leaderboard entry). Code is open.
