Tunguz argues that AI inference spending is about to overtake the database market — the defining software category of the 2010s — making inference "the most important market in software." The numbers: ~$25b paid to run AI models in 2025, ~$130b this year (near Gartner's $161b database forecast), and ~$350b by 2027, roughly 2x databases' $190b.

The consequence is that every application becomes an "inference reseller," and that rewires unit economics three ways. First, usage-based inference bills will dwarf seat licenses, breaking AE comp plans and forcing predictable customer plans against blown budgets. Second, gross margins compress below the classic ~72% SaaS blend unless a vendor builds a proprietary harness to compress token overhead or model competition drives prices down faster. Third, BYOK (bring-your-own-key) customers trade revenue for margin — the vendor books nearly pure software margin on a much smaller contract.

He then asks whether an AI price war might avert this. Capability-adjusted token costs fall ~47% per quarter, but frontier flagship list prices keep resetting upward with capability leaps — the blended frontier price rose from $0.96 to $11.25 in 18 months before GPT-6 Sol reset the tier to $4.00. The upshot: infrastructure cost becomes a major COGS line for the application layer, and software companies become like long-haul truckers watching diesel prices, hunting efficiency with smaller models, better harnesses, and routing.
