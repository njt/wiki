A ~45-minute conference panel (ytx gist: pre-written digest plus full transcript), very likely Craft 2026 — the transcript references Snowden keynoting the previous day on "constructors and scaffolding" and a "Simon" keynote that same morning. Nigel Thurlow (ex-Toyota) moderates Dave Snowden (Cynefin, anthro-complexity), Diana Montalion (systems architect, author of *Mind Shift*), and Daniel North, who introduces himself as "a happy consumer of both complexity work and systems thinking work."

Snowden's central claim is a succession argument: the field is two languages, "systems thinking and complexity science," and complexity science did to systems thinking what quantum mechanics did to Newton — "Newtonian physics is not invalidated by quantum mechanics, but it's bounded." He maps the genealogy: general systems theory and cybernetics, with cybernetics splitting into Ashby's data-signal information (the foundation of modern systems thinking) versus Bateson's abduction and metaphor. System dynamics (Forrester → Meadows → Senge) is the popular branch and the main target — limited feedback loops, archetypes that harden into categories — while soft systems (Checkland) works unconstrained but is facilitation-bound and cannot scale. Complexity itself splits into Santa Fe computational complexity ("abstractions without context") and anthro-complexity: stimulate the system, manage only actants and interactions, and shift energy gradients as patterns stabilize. His sharpest jab — "systems thinking in the main is focused on design without emergence" — is immediately and explicitly rejected by Montalion.

The applied arguments follow. Cynefin's domains are "aspects of a thing rather than categories you put a thing into" (North's "massive epiphany"), and root cause is domain-dependent: ordered systems have root causes, complex systems have "no linear material causality," and RCA there produces retrospective coherence and makes things worse. "All management methods start in manufacturing" — software development still assumes a manufacturing model. Agile committed two heresies: empiricism reduced to "what I did on my last three projects" (real empiricism is hypothesis validation), and reductionism understood as "assuming that the whole can be derived from the parts" rather than decomposing "to the lowest level of coherent granularity." Ashby's Law is a design constraint, not gravity — it rests on Shannon's restricted information theory and can be reversed (Snowden's post-9/11 work used citizens as sensor networks against asymmetric threats). Montalion dissents where the two traditions don't: she writes for individuals because individual thinking is what blocks complexity engagement.

The AI close converges on a distinction both Snowden and Montalion reach independently: AI adds "complication, not complexity." Humans reason abductively, AI inductively, and "everybody is writing software based on inductive reasoning." North's rant: LLMs are "a really, really useful translation tool at scale" that is "always hallucinating," and the money flooding them starves the rest of AI. The gist's own digest is notably honest about what the panel left unresolved — the opening definition question never answered, the mirror critique of Cynefin's own misuse as quadrants dropped, the moderator's question to leaders on AI never delivered, and no method offered for the panel's central prescription of abductive software.
