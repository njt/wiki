Chundru et al. (arXiv:2609.11878, submitted 10 Sep 2026) present a response-level hallucination detection pipeline for LLM output that combines three signals: a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3 classifier, Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty quantification, and temperature-scaled calibration.

On the HaluEval benchmark the pipeline reaches F1=0.915 and AUROC=0.977 on general-domain tasks (per-task F1: 0.97 QA, 0.96 summarisation, 0.82 dialogue), with MC Dropout inference pushing accuracy to 93.2%. An ablation removing the knowledge context drops summarisation F1 by 24%, which the authors read as evidence the detector performs genuine entailment reasoning rather than exploiting surface patterns. A learning-curve analysis finds 25% of training data captures 77% of full-data performance.

Beyond detection, they apply Direct Preference Optimisation (DPO) to a Qwen2.5-0.5B generator, cutting its hallucination rate from 85.5% to 37.7% (a 55.9% relative reduction) as measured by their own detector — detector and fixer feeding each other. Cross-domain transfer is the weak point: general-domain training scores only F1=0.52 on the biomedical SciFact benchmark, and PubMedBERT fine-tuned on SciFact does better at F1=0.63 / AUROC=0.81, motivating domain-specific fine-tuning as the strongest adaptation strategy. Code and models are released publicly.
