KumoRFM-2 is the second iteration of Kumo AI's pre-trained "relational foundation model" — a transformer that does in-context learning directly over multi-table relational databases rather than a single flattened table. It models the database as a temporal heterogeneous graph (rows as nodes, foreign keys as edges, timestamps for time-consistent snapshots) and predicts churn, demand, fraud and similar temporal tasks from a few labeled context examples, with no task-specific training.

Architecturally it processes three scales at once: row/column attention within tables, foreign-key attention across tables, and cross-sample attention across context examples. Task information is injected early ("task-conditioned" processing), which the authors credit for sharper selection of relevant columns and much better noise tolerance than v1. A hierarchical attention scheme avoids quadratic cost over every table cell.

The headline empirical claim: on 41 tasks across RelBenchV1/V2, SALT and 4DBInfer, the few-shot model surpasses supervised approaches — including the task-specific RelGNN by 5% on RelBenchV1 and giant AutoGluon ensembles by 8% on the SAP SALT ERP benchmark — using as little as 0.2% of the available training data as context. The authors state this is the first time a few-shot foundation model has beaten supervised methods on common relational benchmarks. Fine-tuning adds another 16%, completing in under two minutes per task. Robustness studies show strong performance under cold start, link scarcity (up to 75% of edges removed), feature noise, and sparsity.

The system wraps the model in an agent-ready platform: a declarative Predictive Query Language (PQL) that "turns predictive modeling into a composable primitive for agents," automatic context generation by replaying historical database states, pushdown of computation to SQL databases, a memory-mapped graph engine scaling to 500B+ rows, and a shipped set of agent skills plus an MCP server.
