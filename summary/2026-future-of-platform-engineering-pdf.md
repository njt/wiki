Octopus Deploy's 2026 survey report on Platform Engineering: 379 technical practitioners plus follow-up interviews, with sponsorship framing throughout (vendor research, but methodologically careful — they publish tests, p-values, and their own overturned hypotheses).

The headline: platform success is organisational, not technical. The obstacles that actually separate low-performers are a lack of clear strategy, budget, and skills — not tooling sprawl or technical complexity, which teams report at similar rates regardless of performance. Most provocatively, mandatory platform adoption tracks strongly with high performance (62.2% high-performers vs 27.5% under optional), directly contradicting the industry's "platform as a product, adoption must be voluntary" consensus. The authors' reconciliation: mandatory answers the performance question, optional answers the quality question — the goal is a platform that would survive being made optional, with policies mandatory and platforms optional.

Other findings: automation has jumped from fourth to first as the adoption motivation within a year (parallel to the AI shift; 78.4% of organisations reprioritised toward AI), platform teams' median feature scope jumped from 4 to 23 in one year, buy beats build (51.2% use underlying tools, and building from scratch correlates with lacking a strategy — custom building as an unacknowledged substitute for one), 3+ year-old platforms are 2.4x more likely to be high-performers, weekly feedback gathered in-workflow (code reviews/PRs) is the sweet spot, and onboarding speed does not correlate with goal delivery. AI amplifies good platforms rather than fixing bad ones: code coverage, ephemeral environments, and cost control are the features that shift teams from "no AI impact" to positive.
