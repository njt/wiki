# How to Remove Mould from Clothing and Stop It Growing in Wardrobes and Drawers

**Author:** Kellie Scott (for ABC)
**Publication:** RNZ Lifestyle / Home and Garden
**Date:** 17 March 2026

## Summary

The article explains why mould grows on clothing — especially in humid climates and overcrowded storage — and offers expert-backed advice on removal and prevention.

## Expert Sources

1. **Nisa Salim** — associate professor and director of Swinburne-CSIRO National Testlab for Composite Additive Manufacturing
2. **Ash Boss-Handley** — certified occupational hygienist with the Australian Institute of Occupational Hygienists

## What Causes Mould on Clothes?

Salim explains that mould needs the right mix of "moisture, warmth and suitable nutrients" to flourish. Wardrobes and drawers often supply all three. Clothing left slightly damp or carrying sweat, body oils, or food residue provides the organic material mould feeds on. Overcrowding restricts airflow, trapping humidity.

Boss-Handley notes that many fabrics contain organic materials like cotton, wool, silk, or cellulose fibres — "which mould can use as a nutrient source." The result is a musty smell or visible mould patches.

## Types of Mould and Health Risks

Common household moulds like *aspergillus* and *penicillium* species thrive in wardrobes. For clothing, the main risk is structural damage — enzymes digest natural fibres, weakening fabric and leaving stains or odours.

Health-wise, "most species mainly cause irritation, allergies or respiratory symptoms in some individuals." Those with asthma, weakened immune systems, or prolonged exposure may face more significant effects. Boss-Handley stresses that visible mould indoors signals excess moisture and should be dealt with.

## How to Remove Mould from Clothing

Salim's recommended steps:

- Take garments **outside** and brush off loose fungal particles to avoid spreading spores indoors
- Wash with **warm water and appropriate detergent** to detach fungal cells
- For durable fabrics, use **oxygen-based bleach** to kill remaining fungi and remove stains
- Natural fibres like wool or silk need **gentler cleaning** but should still be thoroughly washed and dried

Boss-Handley offers a framing analogy: cleaning mould from a hard surface is like wiping mud off a dinner plate, but cleaning it from a porous textile is more like removing ink from a sponge — "you can remove it from the surface, but the material deeper in the structure may remain."

Once mould is visible on porous materials, fungal root structures can penetrate deep into the fabric. Complete removal is not always possible. Heavily impacted items may need to be discarded, especially if staining, odour, or recurring contamination persists.

## Cleaning the Storage Space

Salim recommends:

1. **Empty** the wardrobe or drawers
2. **Vacuum** surfaces to remove dust and fungi
3. **Wipe hard surfaces** with a mild household disinfectant, diluted vinegar solution, or alcohol-based cleaner

For porous materials like unfinished timber or particle board, mould may penetrate internally. Salim suggests a slightly damp cloth followed by thorough drying, though it may not fully work. Boss-Handley says replacement of the affected material may sometimes be necessary.

## Safety During Cleaning

Boss-Handley advises wearing a **P2 respirator, disposable gloves, and eye protection** to reduce spore exposure. For extensive mould growth, engage a professional mould remediation specialist.

## Prevention Strategies

- Ensure clothes are **completely dry** before storing
- **Improve airflow** by periodically opening wardrobe doors and encouraging cross-ventilation
- Use **moisture absorbers or dehumidifiers** in humid environments
- Choose **breathable garment bags** made from cotton over airtight plastic
- **Avoid overcrowding** wardrobes to allow air circulation
- **Address moisture sources** such as leaks or condensation

Boss-Handley's closing advice: "If in doubt, contact an expert such as an occupational hygienist or indoor environmental professional (IEP) to assess the environment and provide tailored advice."
