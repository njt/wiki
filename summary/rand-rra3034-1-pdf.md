A RAND report (Technology and Security Policy Center) that takes the 2023 Center for AI Safety statement — "mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority" — seriously as an analytical problem. It proceeds from a falsifiable hypothesis: there is no describable scenario in which AI is conclusively an extinction threat to humanity, where "extinction" is defined strictly as the death of every human (deliberately excluding disempowerment and ordinary global catastrophe).

Three scenario chapters test the hypothesis. **Nuclear war**: nuclear winter is unlikely to be truly extinct-causing (insufficient soot in worst-case models; newer simulations predict milder effects than the 1980s originals), and fallout cannot irradiate enough habitable land with existing arsenals; the authors could find no plausible way for AI to overcome existing constraints to cause extinction. **Biological pathogens**: a multi-pathogen pandemic of engineered high-lethality agents is a genuine extinction threat and a potential falsification of the hypothesis — but it would require AI to acquire, design, weaponize, deploy, and then hunt down isolated survivor communities; the authors cannot rule it out. **Malicious geoengineering**: mass-manufacturing super-greenhouse gases to render the Earth uninhabitable is a true extinction threat and physically feasible, though extremely difficult, and it is unclear how AI would be instrumental — the actor would need to control major chemical manufacturing while evading global monitoring.

Methodologically, the report argues that subjective probability estimates of AI extinction are improper under Knightian uncertainty, and distinguishes deep uncertainty (pathway describable, probabilities unknowable) from recognized ignorance (pathway itself indescribable — nanotech, unknown technologies — where watch-and-wait is the only sane strategy and a "shut it down" AI governance approach is inappropriate given AI's benefits). Cross-cutting findings: extinction threats require overcoming immense constraints and unfold over long, observable timescales, allowing response; and any AI actor would need four instrumental capabilities — integration with cyber-physical systems, survival without human maintainers, the objective of extinction, and the ability to persuade or deceive humans — which serve as the report's proposed risk indicators. Recommendations favor resilience-building and trigger-based policy over prediction: spend on things that mitigate extinction risk *and* catastrophe and safety generally, rather than attempting cost-benefit analysis on unquantifiables.
