Alex Ewerlöf — an early adopter who both uses and builds LLM coding systems — writes a long, combative rebuttal to the "coding is solved" narrative pushed by Anthropic's Boris Cherny, DHH, and the "taste is the new leverage" crowd. His core claim: creation is cheap now, but software's real cost lives in maintenance, reliability, security, and the non-functional requirements — and AI cannot be held accountable for any of them.

He argues coding is "one of the last areas for the current generation of LLMs to take over" because code is logic: the only reason LLMs work at all is the harness feedback loop that feeds syntax and runtime errors back until they're solved or hidden. He concedes LLMs are excellent for personal software, POCs, throwaway automation, and map-reduce on human language — all high-risk-tolerance contexts — and useless for the low-risk-tolerance domains (health, finance, aviation) where accountability is the product.

The essay's sharpest sections take aim at specific fallacies: "spec is code" (you can't spec software meaningfully ahead of time), "English is the new programming language" (natural language is vague and conflicting, which is why we built compilers), "I've stopped reading code" (you're confessing to being redundant), and "taste is the new leverage" (everyone has taste; the market doesn't pay for it). His ownership framework — Knowledge, Mandate, Accountability — makes the accountability point concrete: ship it, you own it, regardless of how it was produced. "Slow is fast."

On economics, he argues SaaS companies are increasingly selling SLAs, not code, and that vendors "charging human rates for AI output" face a reckoning: either accept a price crash or justify the price with quality that experienced humans deliver. He ends with practical warnings — beware "AI overdose," keep an AI-free hobby project, be careful about handing your hard-won knowledge to cloud vendors who need it as training data — and predicts engineers will split into technical PMs, AI managers, AI deployment engineers, and AI quality engineers.
