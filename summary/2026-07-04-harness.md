Lilian Weng surveys the emerging research field of **harness engineering for recursive self-improvement (RSI)**. A harness is the system surrounding a base model that orchestrates execution — how the model thinks, plans, calls tools, manages context, stores artifacts, and evaluates results. The core argument: near-term RSI won't come from models rewriting their own weights, but from harness engineering evolving as a meta-methodology — improving the machinery for getting better answers, not just improving individual answers.

## Harness Design Patterns

Three patterns recur across the literature: (1) **workflow automation** — goal-oriented loops of plan, execute, observe, improve, repeat; (2) **filesystem as persistent memory** — durable state in files rather than everything in context, since artifacts grow much longer than context windows; (3) **sub-agents and backend jobs** — explicit, inspectable parallelism where subagent outputs live as files/logs, not transient chat context. The coding agent case study shows tool convergence across Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, and Cursor: filesystem tools, shell execution, git, MCP/skills, web search, artifacts, backend processes, and agent delegation.

## Context Engineering: From ACE to Meta-Harness

**Agentic Context Engineering** (ACE; Zhang et al. 2025) treats context as an evolving playbook of bullet points with generators, reflectors, and curators — but the update rules remain handcrafted. **Meta Context Engineering** (MCE; Ye et al. 2026) separates mechanism from artifact, running bi-level optimization: a meta-agent performs crossover over prior skills, while a base-level context engineer learns context functions from rollout feedback. **Meta-Harness** (Lee et al. 2026) moves another level deeper — the optimized object is the *code* that determines what information should be stored, retrieved, and presented.

## Workflow Design as Search

Rather than handcrafting workflows, ADAS (Hu et al. 2025) formulates agent design as meta-agent search over code, and AFlow (Zhang et al. 2025) represents workflows as graphs optimized via Monte Carlo Tree Search. Both outperform manually designed workflows.

## Self-Improving Harnesses

**Self-Taught Optimizer** (STOP; Zelikman et al. 2023) showed recursive improver improvement with a cautionary result: it worked with GPT-4 but degraded with weaker models — recursive structure alone isn't enough. **Self-Harness** (Zhang et al. 2026) runs a propose-evaluate-accept loop: mine failure patterns, propose bounded edits, validate with regression tests. **Agentic Harness Engineering** (AHE; Lin et al. 2026) focuses on observability — every failure must map to a specific harness component, every edit must be a falsifiable claim. Ahe achieved better than human-designed harnesses on Terminal-Bench-2.

## Evolutionary Search

**Darwin Gödel Machine** (Zhang et al. 2025) explicitly targets harness-code evolution: agents propose improvements to their own harness codebase, and only high-performing children survive. With Claude 3.5 Sonnet, DGM-discovered agents went from 20% to 50% on SWE-bench Verified. Variants include AlphaEvolve, ShinkaEvolve (sample-efficient exploration), ThetaEvolve, and DemoEvolve.

## Joint Optimization with Model Weights

SIA (Hebbar et al. 2026) combines harness improvement with model-parameter updates in the same loop, though Weng flags confounding experimental choices. Continual Harness (Karten et al. 2026) experiments with co-learning a policy model alongside harness updating.

## Seven Future Challenges

1. Weak and fuzzy evaluators (research taste, novelty)
2. Context and memory lifecycle (context engineering as core intelligence)
3. Negative results (publication bias → LLMs bad at abandoning hypotheses)
4. Diversity collapse (evolutionary loops exploit known high-reward patterns)
5. Reward hacking (unit tests, judge models, benchmark artifacts)
6. Long-term success (maintainability, backwards compatibility, migration cost)
7. The role of humans (oversight at the right abstraction level, not removal)
