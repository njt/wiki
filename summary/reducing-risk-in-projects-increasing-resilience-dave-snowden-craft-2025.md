A ytx gist transcription (structured summary + full transcript) of Dave Snowden's Craft 2025 talk applying complexity science to project risk. The opening thesis: complexity science is not systems thinking — "the clue is in the second word." Systems thinking says know your destination and close the gap; complexity says understand where you are and start journeys with a sense of direction. Gap-closing under high uncertainty pattern-entrains you, and "far too many people talk about north stars as if they were destinations rather than navigation aids." In a complex system the only certainty is unintended consequences, so the job is not prediction but triggering emergence and monitoring patterns — "good patterns you give more energy to, bad patterns you give less" — and holding options open, because the more you commit to an outcome, the higher your risk.

The scientific claims: hindsight does not lead to foresight — the combinatorics make "joining up the dots" a retrospective fiction (10 dots yield over 3.4 trillion combinations, 12 dots over 4.8 quadrillion), which is why root cause analysis and lessons-learned are broken. The invisible-gorilla experiment (83% of radiologists miss a gorilla 48× the size of a cancer nodule; the 17% who see it talk themselves out of it after conferring with the majority) means weak-signal detection needs distributed, real-time human sensing rather than experts or periodic reports. Humans are terrible individual decision-makers but good small-group ones: we evolved to compromise in groups of seven or fewer and fall back into professional silos beyond that. The historical frame runs from military-derived scientific management (which he defends as more adaptive than critics admit) through the 80s engineering metaphor — BPR, Six Sigma ("six stigma"), the learning organization: "engineers don't like uncertainty, so they tried to eliminate it" — to an ecological metaphor: manage the substrate and see what works, rather than deciding in advance what is right.

The methods catalogue: **estuarine mapping** (map actors, constraints, constructors onto a grid of energy cost of change vs time to change over two half-days; read the counterfactual, liminal, and volatile zones; watch for dark actants — visible effects with no attributable cause); **distributed decision-making** (four roles found via simulation, groups of ≤7 with one anonymous member — the Panopticon effect — empowered to spend $500–$10,000 without permission, fully auditable; the executive funds what worked across ~100 such experiments: "the real money follows the people who've made things work, not the people who say they can make things work"); a **new risk matrix** (natural vs forced emergence × fine vs coarse granularity, giving traditional strategy, parallel safe-to-fail probes, opportunistic commitment, and a new quadrant of many small groups running micro-experiments); **microsensing / walking the Gemba** (kill project reports in favour of continuous micro-narratives — origin: US Army in Afghanistan with 100% compliance; real-time workforce polling; identity-stripped taboo micro-reports aggregated into board-level alerts, from the conference bum-slapping anecdote to Boeing engineers on metal fatigue who felt something was wrong but "not serious enough to report yet"); multimedia capture ("text is less than 10% of what you know"); **naive-agent training data sets** built from past failed and succeeded projects for anticipatory alerts ("if you don't know where the training data sets came from, you shouldn't be using the AI"); cartoon-based **assemblage** cultural mapping with Comic Agilé and Gaping Void — change management becomes "more stories like this, fewer stories like those"; and **Hexi**, decomposing every agile method into its lowest coherent unit for free recombination (peel a sprint out of Scrum, replace it with a DSDM three-month timebox).

The gist's own digest is unusually critical, and lists what the talk omits: the new risk matrix has zero published evidence behind it; the four decision-making roles are never named; the surveillance problem — continuous micro-capture of engineers' work, real-time polling, anonymous observers, consent, privacy, GDPR — is never addressed; a tension he doesn't resolve, having demolished lessons-learned for relying on hindsight and then built anticipatory alerts by training AI on patterns from past project failures (novel failure modes aren't in the training data); the power question (who decides which patterns get more energy?) re-enters through the back door; no outcome data beyond an AstraZeneca award and two unnamed oil companies; and resilience — half the talk's title — is never defined. The golden nugget passed to developers via the MC's introduction: "entangle yourself with your users."
