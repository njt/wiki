# "We (as a society) peaked in the 90s"

Original article by Chris (@chris), published on chris.pagecord.com, February 1, 2026.

## Full Article Text

The author opens by acknowledging this might be nostalgia, but insists the late 90s had a "specific balanced way" that was lost afterward.

### 1. Music
Digital tools assisted musicians (louder, cleaner, faster) but didn't replace human elements. Bands, scenes, and actual skill still mattered. Music "felt human. Rough round the edges, but in a good way."

### 2. The Web
The early internet was "genuinely exciting" — a place for news, niche forums, and rabbit holes. You'd explore, get what you needed, then step away. The author notes: "It wasn't your whole life." Discovery required effort and felt rewarding.

### 3. Technology as Tools, Not Masters
Consumer electronics had no subscriptions, updates, or ecosystems. You bought a CD player, console, or basic phone, owned it outright, and accepted it would eventually die. Products just worked for you in the moment without ongoing demands.

### 4. Photography
Film photos were intentional — limited shots per roll, developed later. The author contrasts this with modern behavior of firing off "fifty versions of your own face." Some turned out badly, some were magic; that uncertainty was part of the value.

### 5. Slower Pace
Waiting was normal and no one panicked about it. The phrase "delayed gratification" didn't need to be coined because patience was simply built into daily life.

### 6. Tangible Work
Hard work produced something you could point to — not just "emails and meetings and vibes." There was physical proof of effort.

### 7. Authentic Passion
You couldn't fake being into something because "it took too much effort." Enthusiasm required showing up and owning it visibly.

### 8. Slow Release Cycles
Albums, games, and software came out gradually, giving them "weight." People lived with things for a while rather than rapidly replacing them.

### 9. Local Businesses Mattered
Small and medium local enterprises weren't lifestyle brands — they were simply work. They created jobs and community, with money circulating locally.

### 10. Privacy and Imperfection
"People didn't know that much about each other" — and that was fine. You could "disappear for a bit," change, or be "half-formed without documenting and faking it."

### Conclusion
The author acknowledges the 90s weren't perfect — "plenty of stuff was grim" — but argues the balance was right: "We used tech without letting it own us." Then, at some point, that equilibrium flipped.
