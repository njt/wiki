Thorsten Ball plants his flag on seventeen predictions about software
development under agents, originally posted on X in September 2026 and
re-posted on his blog as a dated statement of belief. The tone is maximalist:
code review is already dead, unit tests might be next, the terminal dies,
tokens become the new computing paradigm.

The load-bearing claims: humans will review systems and composition, not lines
of code, because they can't find model bugs in a reasonable time anyway. Most
bugs will be "you asked for the wrong thing" bugs, not coding bugs — which
moves the durable value to knowing how to build software (problem framing,
shipping, feedback) while the craft of writing code goes the way of bespoke
shoemaking: "there are still Italian shoe makers around. But look at your
feet." Open source's "given enough eyeballs" logic dissolves because we now
have artificial eyeballs, and "good code" loses its meaning since its
properties were defined by human ergonomics, and humans won't read the
majority of code.

On tooling and economics: most developer tooling is "washed away by tokens"
(he says this as a terminal lover), the PM/Design/Eng triad and Agile die,
startups adopting Google-style process look sillier when agents remove the
constraints those processes managed, and access to tokens becomes the new
gate on who can produce software at all. His closing realism: it will take a
generation, and ASP developers still have jobs — but the model-speed horizon
implies UI generated on the fly, because menus and dashboards are just
human-accessible APIs to dumb machines.
