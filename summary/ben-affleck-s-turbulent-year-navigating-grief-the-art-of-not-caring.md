Ben Affleck interviewed on the One More Question podcast, spanning his tabloid years, grief after losing both parents, why he built Artists Equity, and the sale of his AI video company (Inter Positive) to Netflix. The transcript is an extended meditation on attention, relevance, and presence: he argues that tabloid outrage is ephemeral, that "relevance" is a trap, and that being physically present — for his kids, for the crew, at an office 9-to-5 — is the thing worth optimising a life around.

Key threads:

- **Cultural amnesia and not caring.** Viral rumours (an FBI raid, a burned house) "all turn out to be untrue and nobody remembers it." For someone under constant scrutiny, the relief is that *nothing matters for long* — "tomorrow there'll be somebody else." He explicitly refuses to treat "relevance" as desirable: relevance means being dissected and hotly debated.
- **Grief and the un-fixed life.** Losing his parents two months apart taught him that life does not conform to a three-act structure — "stuff you haven't fixed in life stays unfixed," and his mother's parting observation was that 83 years felt shorter than she expected. Lesson: let go of "the internecine thing, the work conflict, the petty dispute," because none of it is remembered at the end. He chose not to direct a film this year rather than miss his kid's senior year of high school.
- **Artists Equity and fairness.** His production company treats talent as investors with back-end participation rather than up-front fees, aligning incentives across actors and "below the line" crew — a fairness model he wants proven by actually paying out in success.
- **The theatrical question.** He believes theatrical exhibition is real but the bar has risen; Netflix's reach justifies day-and-date for *Animals*, and the audience now has "a sense of authorship" about who makes what and how it reaches them.
- **AI video, at length.** Affleck describes teaching himself CNNs and tensors for VFX, his reaction to seeing transformer video generation ("dude, we're fucked"), then his diagnosis that the labs lacked filmmaking domain expertise — training on scraped H.265 clips instead of natively captioned film data. He built a company around a purpose-built dataset (lidar, volume stages, Unreal) rather than training on peers' work, and sold it to Netflix specifically because Netflix is a guild signatory that can't use the technology without compensating artists. On token costs: subsidising inference as a business strategy is "deciding that you're gonna heat your convenience store by lighting the cash in the register on fire."
- **Fear and prediction.** He dismisses Skynet doom but endorses liability as the real guardrail, and notes that predicting your audience's extinction is a click-driven, evolutionarily tuned product.
