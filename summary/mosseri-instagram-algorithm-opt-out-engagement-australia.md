An opinion piece examining Adam Mosseri's warning, delivered to an Australian press briefing, that switching off Instagram's recommendation system degrades the experience: engagement can fall by as much as 50%, satisfaction scores "fall off a cliff," and a chronological feed gets overwhelmed by brands and publishers.

The article's core move is to invert Mosseri's frame. He offers falling engagement, time-on-platform, and satisfaction as evidence of harm; the author notes that Australia's draft Digital Duty of Care bill exists precisely because legislators believe engagement and time-on-platform *are* the harm. The number he presents as a warning is the outcome the bill is trying to produce.

Two further threads: the brands-and-publishers crowding is real but self-inflicted — Instagram's ranked feed rewarded high-volume posting, and removing ranking does not remove the behaviour it trained. And the one number that would settle the argument is absent: Mosseri gives engagement, time, and satisfaction but never wellbeing measures, regret, or how people feel afterwards — the very metrics Meta has repeatedly been asked about.

The piece closes on Europe: Article 38 of the Digital Services Act has required a non-profiling feed option since 2023 with modest adoption, which industry reads as disinterest. Mosseri's 50% figure reframes that modesty as a measure of how well the option is hidden.
