Scaman dissects Palantir's engagement model — embedding its people (Forward Deployed Engineers) inside client organisations for months or years, distrusting stated requirements, diagnosing the real problem from within — and argues it is the future of strategic work, applied here to the advertising agency business. The software is not the product; "embedded cognitive capacity" is, and the moat is a "private pattern library of the world."

The flywheel mechanism is the split between FDEs who build bespoke, hacky solutions in the client's mess, and Product Development engineers who generalise those solutions into reusable modules. Every engagement makes the whole smarter. Indie practitioners doing embedded work lack this second layer: they compound knowledge in their heads but cannot productise it, so every engagement is linear rather than exponential.

The essay dwells on the political, almost anthropological skills the model requires — Palantir hands new hires Keith Johnstone's *Impro* because the job is reading rooms, building trust over months, and getting access to data whose gatekeepers depend on not giving it up. Scaman names the uncomfortable parts honestly: the model creates lock-in "dependency by design," and it requires clients desperate enough to tolerate the intrusion. She closes on an open question: what is the substrate that lets accumulated understanding become leverage — AI tooling, shared infrastructure, or internal brand capability?

A reader comment adds the practical constraint: leadership must actually trust the embedded specialist to be "in charge" of their specialty, or nothing changes.
