# Ontologies Come of Age (McGuinness 2003)

A Google Scholar citation record for Deborah L. McGuinness's chapter in *Spinning the Semantic Web* (MIT Press, 2003, pp. 171–194). The record's description excerpt carries the chapter's opening argument: the Web took off only when tools let non-technical people generate pages, but those pages still "preserve much of their early character of being aimed at human consumption" — so search still requires humans to eyeball results pages, and "finding the exact information one is seeking on the Web today is not as easy as one would hope."

The chapter (not fully present here; only the framing excerpt) argues we may be "poised for the next major evolution of online environments": moving from pages written for human readers toward content that programs and automatic readers can use — the semantic web thesis that McGuinness, then at Stanford KSL, spent the decade arguing for. The chapter is one of the standard statements of the ontology-as-shared-vocabulary position that fed OWL and the whole 2000s knowledge-representation wave.

As a source this is thin — a citation page, not the chapter text — but it anchors the same lineage as [[Ontology Development 101 (Noy & McGuinness 2001)]]: machine-readable shared specifications as the precondition for software agents that can act on the Web's content rather than just render it.
