Kadhir Manickam, a decade-deep product builder, argues that the obsession with accelerating software engineering is misguided because the real bottleneck is human attention — on both sides of the market. A hypothetical: even if you could ship ten new features a week forever, that would be bad for the business, because most platforms run on one or two core features and win by focusing, not by flooding.

His prescription is that the whole business must speed up together, because what limits growth is the buyer's willingness to pay attention. Shipping a valuable feature requires validation, iteration, conversion, pricing, legal, and more — "more AI" doesn't fix a business and process problem. The essay closes on why people obsess over engineering speed anyway (it's the problem with the shortest feedback loop) and advises channelling attention to the highest-conviction, needle-moving bets.
