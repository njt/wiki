Cristina White transcribed and categorised all 178 fiction-writing notes from her two-year low-residency MFA notebook: terse, numbered aphorisms sorted into characters, dialogue, endings, general craft, plot, reading, revision, sentences, setting, structure, and writing wisdom. The notes come from many different teachers, so the collection deliberately includes contradictions — the author flags at least one place where advice from one teacher directly contradicts another.

The strongest recurring thread is embodiment: "characterization is action," "the body is the apparatus of the plot," abstractions like "she's traumatized" are useless, and only concrete, specific, surprising detail carries meaning. A second thread is revision as the actual art — "the art is in the revision, it's not punishment," scaffolding drafts, ruthless cutting, three trusted readers and the rule that if two agree, concede.

The wisdom section is psychological rather than technical: tolerate your own bad writing, self-trust is the number-one skill for a novel, don't write toward the haters, and the closing joke that after two years of note-taking you should "forget all your notes." The author notes she paid $35k for this advice and is giving it away.
