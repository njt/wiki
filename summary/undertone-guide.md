Undertone (undertone.guide) is a web tool that elicits a person's visual taste through guided pairwise comparisons — "This one" / "Hard no" over sample design references — and compiles the results into a written creative brief a designer can act on. The fetched source is a client-rendered single-page app; what survives is the first-screen capture (a mock editorial magazine layout being compared) plus the tool's UI copy extracted verbatim from its JavaScript bundle.

The substance of the page is the tool's own epistemology, stated unusually carefully. It repeatedly disclaims its own inferences: explanations are "competing provisional explanations, not measured traits or calibrated confidence"; whole-reference choices are "weak, confounded clues"; the evidence score is "uncalibrated, not a probability"; completing the guided pass "does not imply that every preference is understood." The comparison dimensions are concrete design variables — layout compositions, typefaces (Space Grotesk, Bodoni Moda, Jost, Syne, Cormorant Garamond), colour palettes, image treatments and print finishes (uncoated grain, laid paper, blind emboss), and full sample applications.

The product logic: a guided, budgeted comparison pass (with defined exit conditions for no-preference, double-rejection, and like-both outcomes) → held-out checks where fresh pairs test predictions made from earlier choices → a shortlist of named references plus explicit boundaries and notes → an editable written direction "ready to discuss with your designer." The user's written notes take precedence over the model's feature explanations throughout.
