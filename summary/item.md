A Hacker News comment by zero_shift reflecting on trying to trace the lineage of a workplace decision — an AI feature shipped in a product — and finding no human at the origin. The code was written by Claude from a prompt, the prompt came from an Atlassian-AI-generated ticket, the ticket came from AI-digested docs, the docs came from strategy memos that were "90% sure written entirely by Claude," and the strategy itself was chosen by execs who now communicate mostly through AI-written memos and justify decisions by citing "tech influencers, market conditions, customer expectations."

The author concludes that nobody in the ecosystem is actually in control: several layers of human review exist nominally, but the actual source of the decision can't be pinned down. The feature wasn't built because anyone wanted it, but "because we thought other people expected it" — and those expectations trace back to market hype fed by investors who don't understand the technology, running in a speculative feedback cycle.

The closing move is the sharpest: the situation "ironically... rather resembles the kind of 'misaligned' superintelligence we are supposed to be avoiding" — work oriented by inscrutable machines and inscrutable incentives rather than any concrete human intention. It has, in the author's phrase, "all been hollowed out."
