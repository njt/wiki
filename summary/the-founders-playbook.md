A practical guide from Anthropic for founders building startups where AI is
core infrastructure. The playbook maps the four startup stages — Idea, MVP,
Launch, and Scale — to the realities of 2026, where agentic coding,
conversational research, and workflow automation let founders ship with teams a
fraction of traditional size. Its central claim: AI has dissolved the wall between
"people who can build" and "people with ideas worth building." The founder's
role shifts from individual contributor to orchestrator of AI agents, directing
what to build and why while AI handles construction and operations.

Each stage gets distinct treatment. **Idea** centers on disciplined validation
before building — using Claude as devil's advocate to pressure-test hypotheses,
run competitive mapping, and design customer discovery interviews. The
playbook warns that agentic coding's speed makes it dangerously easy to skip
validation and mistake a working prototype for evidence of problem-solution
fit. **MVP** focuses on translating validated problems into minimal products
while avoiding *agentic technical debt* — codebases built without architectural
context that drift across sessions — and false PMF signals. The prescription:
write CLAUDE.md context files before coding, define scope boundaries, and run
security reviews before any user touches the product. **Launch** shifts from
proving the product deserves to exist to proving the business deserves to grow:
remediating technical debt, building systems that replace founder attention, and
standing up compliance and product management processes. **Scale**
re-centers the founder as public-facing executive, building defensible moats
through domain expertise encoded into the product, compounding user data, and
creating workflow lock-in via deep integrations.

Throughout, the playbook distinguishes three Claude surfaces: Chat for quick
exchanges, Claude Cowork for multi-source knowledge work and operational
automation, and Claude Code for agentic software development. Concrete
exercises accompany each stage. The closing thesis: the founder's job hasn't
changed — find a real problem, build something that solves it, scale it into a
company that matters — but what's changed is the path. "The bottlenecks are no
longer what you can build, but what you choose to build."
