A Hacker News commenter in their 40s describes giving up on posture for most of their life because the only advice on offer — "stop slouching" — produced a puffed-chest position they could never sustain. The breakthrough came from an unexpected direction: chi running videos, from a school they never took up as an actual running practice, supplied the two cues that finally made good posture executable.

The cues: (1) push the crown of the head to the sky as if being pulled by a string, and (2) engage the core so that the pelvis is level. The commenter flags the second as the key insight — they had never engaged their core when sitting or standing, so "standing straight" had no structural foundation and always collapsed.

For sitting, they keep an ergonomics reference picture at the desk as a re-evaluation prompt. And to combat the effects of decades of forward head posture, they do daily chin tucks from the McKenzie method for neck pain and injury prevention.
