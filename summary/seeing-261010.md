A personal field report from the anti-LLM side of software culture, dated 2026-10-10. The author surveys what "people who care about the art of software" are actually doing as generative AI saturates the industry: not debating whether AI is good, but building alternatives and organising.

The essay has three movements. First, normalising "pro-human discussion" — asserting that the practical merits of a tool cannot outweigh the moral ones, with an explicit anti-slop aesthetic (AI-generated video as "cheap sugar and MSG"). Second, community-building as resistance: the author's friend's ground-up Forth-on-x86 system (Evocation), the Polyphony community growing from one protocol into a small organisation with a second project (slopscore), and the argument that "programming is labor" and context lives in developer brains, so the work cannot pause. Third, political economy: the author has drifted away from large commons like nixpkgs toward small, aligned projects, arguing that "the ruling class understands software rules the world" and will happily take slop produced by developers who don't care about ethics.

The piece ends with concrete hope rather than pure grievance: David Bushell's website, the Website League, Fluxer's federation plans, a DIY electrolysis machine with Rust firmware, Forgejo instances in the Dutch government, and the French state running NixOS. The closing line: "I look forward to writing complicated software. The world seems tired of one-more-SaaS."
