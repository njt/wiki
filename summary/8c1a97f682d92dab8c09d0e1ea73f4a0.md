Evan Boyle's "autonomous-delivery" Agent Skill (v2.1.0, MIT, distributed as a gist with SKILL.md + README) encodes an *adaptive* orchestrator-worker methodology: a balanced outer coordinator holds the user's intent and delegates outcomes to direct workers or local orchestrators, then periodically reflects on the workflow itself — task boundaries, specialization, context, hierarchy, concurrency — and reshapes it based on evidence.

The core discipline is model selection by leverage: strongest reasoning for design decisions, hard bugs, and high-risk review; fast capable workers for bounded work with clear contracts. "Increasing all three [capability, reasoning effort, context] for every call is not a strategy." Delegation passes down scope and budget; a recipient may narrow its envelope, never widen it. Optimizing metric is verified useful outcomes per unit of effort, not agent activity.

A second major section covers recurring work via session automation: same-session wakeups (continuing coordinator context) vs. fresh-session scheduled jobs (explicit durable checkpoints), reconcile-before-acting cycles, watermarks over backlogs, and idempotency safeguards — "a timer does not prove a process is stuck."

Five worked examples (small bug, cross-repo feature, uncertain research, broad migration, end-to-end delivery) illustrate that organization follows the task, with explicit boundaries: skills grant no permissions, evidence must match claims, and external writes have unknown outcomes until reconciled.
