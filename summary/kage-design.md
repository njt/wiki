Kage (kage.design) is a design-inspiration gallery whose whole pitch is the last mile between taste and agent: browse "beautiful interfaces from real products and turn any design into a prompt for Claude Code, Codex or Cursor." The homepage counts the offering at 240 designs, 1,265 components, 135 products, 17 skills, and 7 tools — implying the gallery ships agent-side assets (skills, tools) alongside the reference catalog, not just a browsable site.

The visible catalog is dominated by polished consumer landing pages: fintech (ynab.com, wealthfront.com, venmo.com, rocketmoney.com, public.com, monarchmoney.com, lemonade.com, tryobra.com), crypto (phantom.com, ledger.com), AI tools (krea.ai, Loqua, Jackalope, Cadenya), developer tools (Moji), productivity (Raycast 2.0), communication (ChatHop), cloud infra (termsquad.com), food (sweetgreen.com), consumer events (eventbrite.com), and video (descript.com). Every entry is the same shape: a real product's landing page, tagged by category.

The site is a product homepage, not an essay — no author, pricing, or methodology for how a design becomes a prompt is visible in the fetched body. What it signals is the interesting part: visual reference is being sold as an agent-consumable input. The unit of design inspiration is no longer a mood board a human designer studies; it's a prompt artifact aimed at a coding agent.
