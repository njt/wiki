# Your Backlog Has a Hierarchy Problem

ProdPad blog (undated, fetched 2026-09-08).

## Summary

The flat backlog is a structural failure, not a clutter problem. When "replace the payment system" and "add a button to this page" sit in the same list as peer-level items, every prioritization conversation has to start from scratch — because no hierarchy says whether you're comparing apples to oranges, or oranges to aircraft carriers.

The piece opens with a Head of Product describing their backlog as "a graveyard with a search bar": 400+ items accumulated over two years, all tagged "ideas," from multi-quarter platform bets to vague sentences pasted out of Slack. Every sprint became a ritual of scrolling, arguing, and picking based on whoever made the strongest case that week.

The diagnosis: a flat backlog implies every item deserves the same kind of evaluation. It doesn't. Different-altitude work requires different stakeholders, time horizons, risk assessments, and evidence thresholds. Under flat conditions, teams fall into three dysfunctional patterns — volume-based prioritization (noise beats strategy), recency bias (the backlog becomes a queue), and false equivalence in scoring (RICE produces a number that looks like rigor but is "fiction dressed up as analysis").

The fix borrows **Klaus Leopold's flight levels** (strategic / coordination / operational) and maps them to three backlog tiers: **objectives** (where are we going), **initiatives** (what problem, what outcome), and **ideas** (how might we solve it). Prioritization happens at the right altitude — initiatives against initiatives, ideas against ideas within a parent initiative — so comparisons become like-for-like.

Two supporting moves: a **two-backlog principle** (a product/opportunity backlog upstream of delivery, separate from a committed delivery backlog), and a critique of delivery tools (Jira epics are "buckets," not strategic constructs, and pull the conversation downward into tickets and story points).

The closing thesis: a backlog is not a to-do list; it is a **decision system**. Hierarchy is the precondition for good prioritization — the thing that makes any scoring framework or roadmapping process effective in the first place.
