talat is a commercial meeting-transcription app for Mac (Apple Silicon, macOS 15+) and Windows that runs entirely on-device: dual-stream meeting capture, hotkey dictation into any app, and import of existing audio/video, with transcription via hardware-accelerated local models (Qwen3.5-4B built in, or bring your own) and a local database that "never leaves your machine."

The pitch is privacy by architecture rather than policy: cloud LLMs are optional for summaries (the site concedes they "usually give better results on longer meetings"), supplied with the user's own API key and provider choice. Notable agentic touchpoint: an MCP connector so meetings land "in your AI tools" — one testimonial praises the Claude workflow via MCP, calendar integration, speaker detection, and cross-meeting voice prints that stay on-device.

A comparison table positions talat against Granola, Otter.ai and Plaud on where audio is processed, whether a meeting bot joins calls, whether voice prints and training data stay local, offline capability, and pricing ($9/mo or $189 lifetime, no account). Distribution is indie: a team of two, 10-hour free trial, no accounts, one-time license option, and the explicit claim that "your money funds an independent product built to serve its users rather than its investors (who don't exist)."
