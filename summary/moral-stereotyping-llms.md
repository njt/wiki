This paper asks whether LLMs can accurately estimate moral values across 48
nations, and finds they cannot — they systematically stereotype. The authors
queried various GPT versions about the "average" person from each country and
compared the results to real Moral Foundations Questionnaire (MFQ-2) survey
data from n=90,802 people.

LLMs consistently overestimated Care and underestimated Purity across cultures,
and showed a Western bias: they inflated moral-concern scores for countries like
the US, Canada, and Australia while underestimating them for Nigeria, Morocco,
and Indonesia. The moral framework used covers six foundations — Care, Equality,
Proportionality, Loyalty, Authority, and Purity.

The authors frame this as an epistemic and ethical risk. Using LLMs to simulate
or estimate public moral opinion — increasingly common in social-science
research — produces distorted results that misrepresent non-Western populations
and reinforce cultural stereotypes. The root cause is presumed to be
WEIRD-heavy training data producing models that encode Western moral norms as
universal defaults.

Data and code are deposited on OSF. The paper appears in *Proceedings of the
National Academy of Sciences* (Vol. 123, No. 10, March 2026).
