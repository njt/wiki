Section 5 ("AI RMF Core") of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0, published January 2023 and read here from the NIST AI Resource Center, where the 2023 text is explicitly marked as being revised. The Core is the framework's operational heart: four functions — **govern**, **map**, **measure**, and **manage** — each broken into categories and subcategories (roughly 60 outcome statements in total). NIST insists the actions "do not constitute a checklist, nor are they necessarily an ordered set of steps," and that risk management should be "continuous, timely, and performed throughout the AI system lifecycle." A voluntary companion Playbook offers tactical suggestions for each subcategory.

**Govern** is the cross-cutting function that "enables the other functions": a risk-management culture, transparent policies, accountability structures with executive ownership, workforce diversity in risk decisions, external engagement, and policies for third-party software, data, and supply-chain risk (including decommissioning systems safely). **Map** establishes context — intended purposes, interdependencies among lifecycle actors, system categorization, benefits and costs, and impacts to individuals and society — and produces the basis for an initial go/no-go decision. Its candid premise is that no single actor has "full visibility or control" over the whole lifecycle, so best intentions in one stage get undermined by decisions in later ones.

**Measure** covers quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method TEVV (test, evaluation, verification, validation): test sets and metrics documented, independent reviewers to counter internal bias, evaluation of safety ("it can fail safely, particularly if made to operate beyond its knowledge limits"), security, privacy, fairness, explainability, and environmental impact — plus tracking of "existing, unanticipated, and emergent" risks and feedback/appeal channels for affected communities. Notably, subcategories must document "the risks or trustworthiness characteristics that will not – or cannot – be measured." **Manage** allocates resources to the mapped and measured risks: prioritized treatment (mitigate, transfer, avoid, or accept), documented residual risk, third-party and pre-trained-model monitoring, mechanisms to "supersede, disengage, or deactivate" misbehaving systems, post-deployment monitoring plans, and incident communication to affected communities.
