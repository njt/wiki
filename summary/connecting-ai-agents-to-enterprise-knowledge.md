MIT Technology Review Insights' sponsored report (with Neo4j), based on a survey of 300 data, AI and technology executives, on why enterprise AI agents stall for lack of *knowledge* — not data, but data understood in organizational context.

Only about a third (34%) of agentic AI projects reach production on average. The report frames this as a knowledge-capability problem across three registers: semantic knowledge (what the data means), episodic memory, and procedural knowledge. A small group of "production leaders" — where 61% of projects pass pilot — correlates with stronger semantic knowledge capability, not bigger models or budgets.

Data fragmentation is the most-cited obstacle to agent knowledge access (55%), though production leaders are more likely to name security and privacy (72%) — an inversion that suggests leaders have already solved fragmentation and moved on to the harder containment problem. The executive consensus on remediation: invest in retrieval (ingestion pipelines, AI-ready APIs, RAG), in AI evaluation agents, and in knowledge graphs — the "structural foundation" linking data to agents, which is of course what the sponsor sells.

Notably thin: the piece is a findings summary, not the full report — no methodology detail, no definitions of how "knowledge capability" was measured, and the correlation between semantic capability and production rates is asserted rather than analyzed.
