SILO-BENCH is a role-agnostic benchmark of 30 algorithmic tasks across three communication-complexity levels, used to run 54 configurations over 1,620 experiments on multi-agent LLM systems. The question it asks: can agents *compute with* distributed information, or only *exchange* it?

The headline finding is a "Communication-Reasoning Gap": agents spontaneously form task-appropriate coordination topologies and exchange information actively, but systematically fail at the reasoning-integration stage — they often acquire sufficient information yet cannot synthesize the distributed state into a correct answer. The failure is localized and diagnosable, not diffuse.

Worse, coordination overhead compounds with scale and eventually eliminates parallelization gains entirely. The conclusion is pointed: naively scaling agent count cannot circumvent context limitations. The benchmark (code available) is offered as a foundation for tracking progress toward genuinely collaborative multi-agent systems.
