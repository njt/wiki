Kellan Elliott-McCrea responds to David Bessis's "The fall of the theorem economy," drawing parallels between mathematics and software engineering through two borrowed terms.

**Canonization** (from Alex Kontorovich) is the work of turning a local, one-off formalization into library-quality work: general, reusable, coherent, efficient, and compatible. It's what turns something that works into something the future can build on — the inverse of "vibe coded" throwaway software. Elliott-McCrea frames its absence as "the photo negative of technical debt": features that resist change and drain morale signal missing canonization.

**The Overhang** (from Bessis) is the unrealized latent value sitting in past creative work, waiting to be unlocked by connecting existing ideas. LLMs, capable of memorizing entire corpora and pattern-matching at scale, are well-suited to searching this space in ways human cognition can't.

The core warning: if the industry rewards only production (theorem-proving, coding) while neglecting canonization and the training of new practitioners, the Overhang will deplete. Review and cleanup earn no status; vibing code into existence does. "Generation easy. Canonization hard." The task is to reimagine what teams reward so practices don't leave people or the Overhang exhausted.
