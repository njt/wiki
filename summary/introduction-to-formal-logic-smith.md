Peter Smith's textbook is a rigorous introduction to classical first-order
quantification theory, aimed primarily at first-year philosophy undergraduates.
It starts from scratch — what an argument is, the distinction between deductive
and inductive reasoning, validity vs. soundness — and builds methodically toward
formal proof systems for propositional logic (PL) and quantifier logic (QL, then
QL with identity). The second edition replaces the first edition's truth-tree
chapters with a Fitch-style natural deduction system, though the tree chapters
remain available as online supplements.

The book's architecture is careful and cumulative. Early chapters drill the
reader on what validity means (necessarily truth-preserving inference), how to
spot it, and how to test with the counterexample method, before formalizing
anything. A key early principle is the *invalidity principle*: an inference step
with true premises and a false conclusion must be invalid. The formal half opens
with propositional logic — syntax, truth-table semantics, tautological
entailment, expressive adequacy, and the truth-functional conditional (with
extended discussion of how "if" in English maps — and doesn't — to material
implication). The quantifier half introduces names, predicates, quantifier-variable
notation, QL syntax and semantics, Q-valuations, and Q-validity. Later chapters
add identity and definite descriptions, and a final appendix sketches soundness
and completeness results.

Smith writes in a conversational but mathematically precise style. He calls out
common student mistakes (confusing *use* with *mention*, confusing `→` with
English "if"), explains *why* each formal choice was made (why these three
connectives? why natural deduction?), and provides exercises with detailed worked
answers online. The book is explicitly opinionated: Smith favours a classical,
two-valued conception of logic and takes positions on contested points (e.g., the
material conditional is adequate for mathematical reasoning if not for everyday
conditionals). Throughout, he's transparent that the book is one path among many
into logic — not the final word, but a careful first one.

The freely-available PDF (420 pages) was self-published by Smith at Logic Matters
after Cambridge University Press's second edition went out of print. A third
edition covering both trees and natural deduction was planned.
