The first large-scale empirical study of LLM agents actually running in production: 20 in-depth case-study interviews plus a survey of 306 practitioners, filtered to 86 deployed (production or pilot) systems across 26 domains, collected April–November 2025.

**Why agents get built (RQ1).** 80% deploy for productivity gains, 72% for reduced human task-hours. 92.5% of agents serve human users, mostly internal employees (52%) — a deliberate risk choice. Latency tolerance is surprisingly relaxed: 66% allow minutes or longer, because agents beat human baselines by 10x even at that speed.

**How they're built (RQ2).** Production agents favour simplicity and control: 17/20 case studies use closed frontier models; 70% use off-the-shelf models with no fine-tuning (prompting is more robust to model upgrades and cheaper to maintain); 79% use manually constructed prompts (only 9% use prompt optimizers like DSPy); 68% execute at most 10 steps before human intervention; 80% use predefined structured workflows rather than open-ended planning. 85% of case-study teams build custom in-house implementations instead of frameworks — for flexibility, simplicity, and security.

**How they're evaluated (RQ3).** Human-in-the-loop evaluation dominates (74%); LLM-as-a-judge (52%) is always paired with human review, routing low-confidence outputs to experts. 75% forgo formal benchmarks, relying on A/B tests, user feedback, and golden QA sets grown from production data.

**Top challenge (RQ4).** Reliability (38% rank "core technical performance" first, vs 3% governance). Teams mitigate it through system-level design: read-only modes, sandboxes, internal deployment, role-based access, bounded autonomy — not algorithmic advances. Security is likewise handled implicitly via operational constraints, flagged as an open problem as deployments go external.

The paper's central claim: production reliability comes from systems engineering, not model-level cleverness — a research-practice gap the authors document to redirect research (sample-efficient post-training, observability, bounded autonomy, human-oversight workflows).
