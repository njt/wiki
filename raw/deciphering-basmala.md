# Deciphering basmala

Mark Dominus walks through the typographic and linguistic complexity behind the **basmala** — the Islamic phrase "bismillah al-raḥman al-raḥim" (in the name of God, the gracious, the merciful) that opens 113 of the 114 Qur'anic surahs.

## Background

Dominus recommends first reading a piece by Saleh about Arabic typesetting complications, involving "a twice-mutilated vizier, a Qurʾān that vanished for four centuries, a Beirut newspaperman with a deadline," and an Egyptian physician who taught himself font engineering.

Dominus disclaims: he does not know Arabic and acknowledges potential errors.

## The Rendering Problem

Arabic script is always cursive, so ligature rendering is critical. Unlike Latin script where missing ligatures are "barely noticeable," Arabic text without proper ligatures "looks grossly wrong."

There is "a centuries-long tradition of calligraphic expression of this phrase, in the most perfect possible ways." Rendering it poorly could be seen as blasphemous.

## The Unicode Solution

Unicode codepoint **U+FDFD** `ARABIC LIGATURE BISMILLAH AR-RAHMAN AR-RAHEEM` was created as a single character with one carefully designed glyph, sidestepping the limitations of font rendering engines. He shows it renders differently across platforms — long and narrow in Firefox, very different on Android.

## The Four Words

1. **Bismi** (بسم) — "in the name of" — three letters /b/ + /s/ + /m/
2. **Allah** (اللّٰه) — "God" — placed at the top (traditional)
3. **al-raḥman** (الرحمن) — "the gracious"
4. **al-raḥim** (الرحيم) — "the merciful"

Dominus explains the **kashida** (horizontal stroke for layout), the assimilation of "al-" before /r/ sounds, and notes the survival of "al-" in English words like alcohol, algebra, and alchemy.

## Letter Forms

- **Mīm** (م) — long tail when word-final
- **Rā'** — down-hanging hook (الر)
- **Ḥā'** (ح) — the in-between /h/ sound, losing its "fancy tail" medially
- **Yā'** (ي) — in medial position, an "upward-pointing spike with two dots below"
- **Hā'** — the soft /h/, the last letter of Allah

He notes the difficulty of learning Arabic sounds, citing "three sounds that resemble English /h/" of varying roughness.

## Closing

Dominus links to Wikimedia Commons' gallery of basmalas and expresses anticipation for deepening his understanding of them. He calls Arabic script "very beautiful."
