# Dopamine Fracking

**Author:** German S.
**Date:** 2026.04.13
**Read time:** 8 min
**Tags:** dopamine, metaphor, culture, rant

## Full Text

The author defines "dopamine fracking" as forcing enormous resources — money, data, optimization — into a formerly casual activity to "forcefully extract and squeeze out the purest, most concentrated dopamine hit, with no regard for anything except dopamine."

## Origin

The term was coined on Discord. Like actual fracking, the author argues it is "immensely harmful to the long-term health and sustainability of anything it is applied to," though it can yield an intense short-term dopamine (or oil) hit. They briefly considered "sloptimization" but felt it didn't capture the destructiveness. The phrase "dopamine fracking" evokes an "oil rig in your brain, or worse, in things you love and cherish."

## Commodifying the Human Experience

The author was inspired by Metta Beshay's videos on drugs and their original cultural contexts. The argument is that certain substances used for millennia became destructive when "taken out of that context" due to "industrialization and cultural erasure by the Enterprising Capitalist™️."

This pattern extends to culture, hobbies, and relationships online. Communities, music, videos, movies, and websites all chase the dopamine hit. The author notes that "if everything converges on a single point, there's quite literally no room for anything else in zero dimensions."

## The Strawberry Example

A lengthy analogy: strawberries have thousands of strains and unique compounds. The experience of eating one is described as "a beautifully imperfect, unique, analog experience." But the food industry extracts and synthesizes the single compound that smells most like strawberry, replacing the real thing.

This "completely erase[s] everything else" about the experience — texture, juiciness, complexity, imperfections, memories of grandma's jam. The author warns: "Soon enough, you forget what one actually tastes like. Or worse, you prefer the chemicals." This is compared to the SpongeBob episode where Krabby Patties were made from grey goo.

The author concludes this analogy by stating that dopamine fracking does the same to culture, hobbies, and relationships — extracting dopamine while erasing "all the complexity, nuance, and beauty that made it special."

## Conclusion

Optimization was long seen as positive. The author acknowledges participating in it and suspects others have too. They describe turning away from it: "deleting channels and feeds that infuriate me or milk my triggers," uninstalling apps, and setting boundaries. "Becoming aware of this concept has made it easier to navigate the world."

No solutions are offered, but "awareness is the first step." The author hopes people discuss this phenomenon, even if not using the term "dopamine fracking," noting that "we call short-form sludge 'brain rot,' so why not?"
