# Discovery debt: why unvalidated assumptions are more expensive than technical debt

Benedikt Kantus, Leading in Product, June 16, 2026.

## Summary

Discovery debt is the accumulated weight of untested assumptions, unvalidated customer problems, and market insights that teams keep deferring. Every stakeholder-driven feature built without customer validation, every user research session skipped for a deadline, every confident assertion in a room where no one has actually spoken to a user recently — all of this is discovery debt accruing.

The key distinction: discovery debt is "invisible. It doesn't show up in build times or sprint velocity." It surfaces months later, when unused features become load-bearing or a competitor quietly solves the real problem.

> "Technical debt slows you down. Discovery debt sends you in the wrong direction entirely."

## How It Compounds

Discovery debt doesn't grow linearly — it compounds. One unvalidated assumption becomes the foundation for the next decision. Assume users want Feature A, build it, design Feature B to extend it, then Feature C to patch the gap — all three decisions deep without testing the original premise.

Each skipped discovery session makes the next harder to justify, because "stopping to do research feels like going backwards." The product grows larger, harder to navigate, less focused. As the author puts it, you're not just wrong — you're "expensively, intricately wrong."

## Why Smart Teams Accumulate It

Discovery debt builds up in good teams under real pressures: deadlines, impatient stakeholders, sales teams needing demo material, competitive responses to the CEO's requests. The core asymmetry: "The shipped feature, the closed ticket, the updated roadmap: these are legible." The untested assumption, the conversation never had — "these are invisible. Invisible work loses."

There's also the **confidence trap**: the longer you've worked on a product, the more you feel you know your users. But "users change. Markets shift." The mental model from eighteen months ago is a starting point, not a substitute.

## How to Pay It Down

Four actionable approaches:

1. **Protect discovery capacity** — reserve roughly 20% of team time for pure discovery, unconnected to feature milestones
2. **Question one assumption per sprint** — test one thing the team treats as fact, through one conversation, experiment, or data pull
3. **Talk to users who left or never came** — churned or non-converting users yield harder but more valuable insights
4. **Design experiments to prove you wrong** — instead of confirmation-seeking, ask what would need to be true for an assumption to be completely false

The goal is a habit shift: "treating discovery as continuous work, not a phase you completed before development started."

## Which Is More Expensive?

Technical debt is well-understood, tracked, budgeted for. Discovery debt redirects your team. You "move fast, ship confidently, and build something users don't need."

> "Slow is recoverable. Wrong is expensive."

> "The question isn't whether you have discovery debt. You do. Every product team does."
