Accession negotiations are intergovernmental. The General Affairs Council supervises them, and enlargement decisions generally require unanimous agreement among all EU member states.

## Positive Signals

- On June 30, 2026, the European Commission adopted a financial package under Chapter 33 and noted member-state agreement to begin drafting the accession treaty.
- The Council's ad hoc working party for drafting Montenegro's accession treaty met in May 2026.
- Montenegro's 2026–2027 accession program covers 581 acts across all 33 chapters, with 495 planned for 2026.
- On July 16, 2026, Minister Maida Gorčević stated Chapters 14 and 31 were technically ready, and several chapters were before the Commission.
- Parliament elected the final two Constitutional Court judges on July 16, 2026, restoring full composition — Commissioner Marta Kos linked this directly to Chapter 23.
- Montenegro fully met final benchmark requirements for Chapter 19 (social policy and employment).

## Negative Signals / Risks

- **Croatia is the clearest veto risk.** Vijesti reported on July 10, 2026, that every EU member except Croatia had greenlit Chapter 14, held up over cabotage concerns. Croatia had already blocked Chapter 31 over bilateral disputes.
- On June 5, 2026, Croatian PM Andrej Plenković tied progress on Chapters 31, 23, and 24 to preconditions on multiple bilateral issues: Morinj compensation, missing persons, war crimes prosecution, property rights, the Jadran training ship, the Kotor pool name, and border talks.
- **Chapters 23 and 24** are the rule-of-law core. The 2026 Rule of Law Report notes that constitutional amendments require a two-thirds parliamentary majority and that the judiciary faces human-resource and digitalization limits.
- **Chapter 27** (environment/climate) remains administratively heavy; the 2025 Montenegro report found only 38% of the action plan had been implemented.
- Commissioner Kos noted Montenegro could join in 2028 even without closing all chapters by end of 2026, reducing pressure to force difficult closures.

## Analogy to Croatia

Croatia closed 11 chapters in 2010 and reached 28 of 35 by December 2010 before concluding negotiations in June 2011. This suggests fast endgames are possible but argues against 33 as a base case.

## Non-Obvious Insights

1. Montenegro is past opening chapters and into the endgame (treaty drafting, Chapter 33 financial work), which justifies a forecast above linear extrapolation.
2. Croatia's bilateral vetoes on Chapters 14 and 31 are a real constraint that could cap the final count.
