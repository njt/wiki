Dean W. Ball (@deanwball), Jul 17, 2026, 3:05 PM UTC

Ball describes Kimi as a strong model on par with top Q1 2026 models for agentic coding, though "very token hungry" and likely not cheap to run.

He expresses surprise that China permits open-sourcing of such capable models, attributing this roughly 75% to "strategic blindness/lack of AGI-pilledness" and what he calls the CCP being "very Yann Lecun-y" on AI, and 25% to export controls limiting Chinese inference compute plus an export-driven strategy.

He argues open-weight models are "inherently decelerationist" and questions why self-described accelerationists embrace them, suggesting the appeal is that they're "effectively ungovernable."

Ball claims an open-weight-dominant world leads to "full AI communism" — AI as state-provided "digital public infrastructure" — which he calls dystopian. He notes he's "never met an open-weight models advocate who doesn't ultimately concede this is where things end."

He predicts the Trump Administration will generate regulatory FUD around Chinese open-weight models via agency soft law rather than banning open source outright.

Finally, he says such models make the world "a bit more dangerous, but not so much more that you'll really notice" — until they're capable enough that you will.
