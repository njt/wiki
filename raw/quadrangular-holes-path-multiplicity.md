Complex networks provide a unified framework for describing interacting complex systems, from cells and brains to power grids and social media. As a foundational concept in graph theory, paths that link nodes through edges play a central role in network analysis and computation. Recent work has revealed substantial heterogeneity in shortest path multiplicity across synthetic and empirical networks. However, the core structural mechanisms that give rise to such path multiplicity remain largely unexplored. In this paper, we show that path multiplicity is governed by chordless 4-cycles, namely quadrangular holes. We first investigate topological features of extremal graphs that maximize path multiplicity and, surprisingly, find that these graphs are enriched with chordless 4-cycles, suggesting a plausible microscopic mechanism underlying path multiplicity. We then validate this hypothesis across 140 empirical networks and eight classical synthetic networks. Furthermore, we verify the connection between chordless 4-cycles and path multiplicity through target-oriented optimization and theoretical derivations. Our findings reveal how local motifs shape global path multiplicity, with significant potential applications in network design and optimization in various fields.
