## Five Arguments

1. **Technology diffusion follows S-curves, not exponentials.** Citing St. Louis Fed Real Time Population Survey data, the report finds workplace AI use growing "surprisingly smooth" with no nonlinear inflection point. The laptop and internet adoption curves show the same pattern.

2. **Recursive technology ≠ recursive adoption.** AI can self-improve, but economic deployment is gated by physical capital, energy supply, regulatory approval, and organizational change costs.

3. **Productivity shocks are supply shocks, not demand shocks.** AI automation lowers marginal costs, expands potential output, and raises real incomes. Every major technological revolution in history followed this pattern.

4. **Compute cost is a natural economic boundary.** If automation demand pushes marginal compute cost above labor cost, substitution loses economic meaning.

5. **AI is more likely a complement than a substitute.** The Microsoft Office analogy: feared as a replacement for clerical workers, proved to be an augmentation tool. Keynes's 1930 prediction of 15-hour workweeks failed because society chose higher consumption over less work.

## Greater Bay Area Case Study

The QIIA analysis (Zhao Yuqi / Huang Ping) identifies the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area as a demonstration zone:
- Dongguan/Foshan "lighthouse factories" using AI for visual quality inspection create demand for data annotators and AI equipment engineers
- Nanshan District: 1,351 AI enterprises, AI industry value of 44.9 billion RMB (2024)
- Government-provided computing resources, open data, and cross-border data flow policies as enabling infrastructure
