# 20-20-20 Rule: Are These Numbers Justified?

**Authors:** Sophia Johnson and Mark Rosenfield

**Affiliation:** SUNY College of Optometry, New York, New York

**Journal:** *Optometry and Vision Science* (*Optom Vis Sci*), Vol. 100, No. 1, pp. 52–56, January 2023. Epub December 6, 2022.

**DOI:** 10.1097/OPX.0000000000001971

## Significance

Digital device use has "increased substantially in recent years across all age groups" for both work and leisure, with corresponding increases in digital eye strain. A range of management options exist, including "optical, medical, and ergonomic interventions."

## Purpose

The widely promoted "20-20-20 rule" advises taking a break every 20 minutes to look at something at least 20 feet away for 20 seconds. The authors note there is "relatively little peer-reviewed evidence to support this rule." This study aimed to test whether scheduled breaks reduce the adverse effects of device use.

## Methods

- **Subjects:** 30 young participants
- **Task:** A 40-minute cognitively demanding reading task on a tablet, requiring reading random words and identifying those starting with a specific letter chosen by the experimenter
- **Conditions:** The task was performed on four separate occasions with 20-second breaks at different intervals: every 5, 10, 20, or 40 minutes (the 40-minute condition was effectively no break)
- **Measurements:** Pre- and post-task questionnaires about ocular and visual symptoms; reading speed and task accuracy were also quantified

## Results

- A "significant increase in post-task symptoms" compared to pre-task values was found across all four trials (P < .001)
- However, scheduled breaks had **no significant effect** on:
  - Reported symptoms (P = .70)
  - Reading speed (P = .93)
  - Task accuracy (P = .55)

## Conclusions

The authors state: "These results do not support the proposal of using 20-second scheduled breaks as a therapeutic intervention for digital eye strain."
