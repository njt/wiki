# All Of Me — Jazz Guitar Lesson

Comprehensive jazz guitar tutorial by Dirk Laukens covering melody, harmonic analysis, chord-scale choices, and a transcribed solo over the 1931 Seymour Simons & Gerald Marks standard "All of Me."

The harmony moves slowly (most chords last two bars), making it a good tune for beginners.

## Melody

Built from clear, singable phrases moving mostly stepwise — easy to memorize and good for developing feel and phrasing. Guitar tabs and standard notation included. Backing track made with Band in a Box.

## Harmonic Analysis

- **Form:** 32 bars, ABAC structure (two identical A sections)
- **Key:** C major (Real Book version); original 1931 recording is in Bb major
- **Harmonic movement:** Secondary dominants and circle of fifths

### Chord-by-Chord Scale Choices

| Chord | Function | Recommended Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Cmaj7 (bars 1-2) | Tonic (I) | C major / C major bebop (major + b6) |
| E7 (bars 3-4, 9-10, 19-20) | Secondary dominant | A harmonic minor (= E Phrygian dominant / E Mixolydian b9 b13) |
| A7 (bars 5-6, 21-22, 28, 31) | Secondary dominant → Dm7 | D harmonic minor (= A Phrygian dominant) |
| D7 (bars 13-14) | V/V → G7 → C | D Mixolydian (5th mode of G major) |
| Fm7 (bar 26) | Modal interchange — iv minor | F melodic minor |
| Diatonic chords of C | I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi | C major scale |

### E7 — Two Possible Resolutions

**E7 → Am7 (bars 9-10):** Secondary dominant, notated as V/vi. Resolves to the vi chord.

**E7 → A7 (bars 3-4, 19-20):** Extended dominant, notated as V/VI. Resolves to *another* dominant chord rather than directly to a diatonic target.

The I → V/VI movement appears in: Someday My Prince Will Come, After You've Gone, Come Rain or Come Shine, I'll Be Seeing You, Basin Street Blues, I Should Care, On a Slow Boat to China.

### D7 → G7 → C (V/V → V → I)

The classic secondary dominant in C major. D Mixolydian (5th mode of G major) is the scale of choice.

This V/V → V → I movement appears in: Days of Wine and Roses, Don't Get Around Much Anymore, Just Friends.

### Fm7 — Modal Interchange

The iv minor chord (ivm) in C major, borrowed from the parallel minor key (C minor). Creates smooth chromatic voice leading: A (3rd of Fmaj7) → Ab (b3 of Fm7) → G (5th of Cmaj7).

The iv minor appears in: Fly Me to the Moon, How Deep is the Ocean, How High the Moon.

F melodic minor is the scale of choice — it highlights the characteristic Ab (b3) while keeping the natural 6 (D) for a brighter minor sound.

## Bebop Scales

Bebop scales add a passing chromatic tone to align chord tones with downbeats in eighth-note lines:

- **Major bebop scale:** Major scale + b6 (on major chords)
- **Dominant bebop scale:** Mixolydian mode + natural 7 (on dominant chords)
- **Minor bebop scale:** Dorian mode + natural 7 (on minor chords)

## Solo

Transcribed single-note solo built primarily from arpeggios and bebop scales, keeping the harmony clear and the lines simple.
