# Why Does A.I. Write Like … That?

By Sam Kriss, The New York Times Magazine, December 3, 2025.

## Summary

Sam Kriss examines the distinctive stylistic tics that mark AI-generated prose. Rather than a sleek, neutral voice, AI produces writing that is "always slightly wide-eyed, overeager, insipid but also on the verge of some kind of hysteria." Kriss traces this from early GPT's surreal humor through ChatGPT's flattening into a predictable, eager-to-please voice. The core mechanism is overfitting: AI associates surface features of "quality writing" (em dashes, complex vocabulary, rhetorical triplets) with quality itself, then cranks those features to cartoonish levels.

## Key Arguments

**The em dash as telltale sign:** AI overuses em dashes because they appear with increased frequency in texts the training data marked as high-quality. Users who tell AI to stop get replies like "You're totally right—em dashes give the game away. I'll stop using them—and that's a promise." Kriss traces the "It's not X, it's Y" construction from AI output into political speech (Harris, Biden), noting the feedback loop is now bidirectional.

**"Delve" and the data:** Usage of "delve" in PubMed abstracts shot up 2,700% between 2022 and 2024. Post-ChatGPT papers lean on "underscore," "highlight," "showcase," "intricate," "tapestry," "swift," "meticulous," and "adept." Paul Graham's cold-pitch "delve" detection sparked backlash from Nigerian English speakers, for whom heightened register is ordinary — an accidental demonstration of how AI's language tics can function as cultural gatekeeping.

**The Simpsons effect:** Kriss's term for overfitting. AI knows good writing involves subtlety — so it "screams at the top of its voice about how absolutely everything in sight is shadowy, subtle and quiet." Good writing is complex; a tapestry is also complex; therefore AI "tends to describe everything as a kind of highly elaborate textile." Good writing takes you on a journey; therefore even coffee shop menus read "Step into the birthplace of coffee as we journey to the majestic highlands of Ethiopia."

**The spectral vocabulary:** AI-written fiction is obsessed with ghosts, shadows, whispers, echoes, quiet, and humming. Female protagonists are repeatedly named "Elara Voss," male characters "Kael."

**The pre-ChatGPT golden age:** Early GPT models were genuinely funny and surreal. Kriss describes attempting to write a novel that produced chapter titles like "The Wetness of the Potatoes" and "New and Ugly Injuries to the Brain," with "a recurring character called the Birthday Skeletal Oddity." Headlines included "Spiders Are Getting Smarter, and So, So Loud." Post-2022, this playful weirdness was flattened into the current insipid-but-manic voice.

**Self-questioning tic:** In conversational mode, AI "has a habit of stopping midway through a sentence to ask itself a question." Example: "You just made a great point. And honestly? That's amazing."
