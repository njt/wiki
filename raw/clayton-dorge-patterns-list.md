# 🏡 List of 253 Patterns — Clayton Dorge

**Site:** claytondorge.com
**Tagline:** "Bravely Curious"

The author read all of Christopher Alexander's **A Pattern Language** and summarized each pattern via Twitter (@apatterntolearn). This page collects all 253 patterns organized in three groups as described in the book:

- **Patterns 1–94** = Towns
- **Patterns 95–204** = Buildings
- **Patterns 205–253** = Construction

---

## Patterns 1–94: Towns

Described as things "built gradually piece by piece with small individual acts" to fit within larger global patterns.

1. **Independent Regions** — Regions must be small and autonomous enough to be independent cultural spheres.
2. **The Distribution of Towns** — Population balance between villages and cities is critical; too far in either direction leads to ruin.
3. **City Country Fingers** — Sprawl destroys life, but city size remains valuable.
4. **Agricultural Valleys** — Develop on hilltops; steward valleys with ecological responsibility.
5. **Lace of Country Streets** — Country roads at least a mile apart, enclosing farmland squares.
6. **Country Towns** — Smaller towns need local industry for self-sustainability, not just dormitories.
7. **The Countryside** — Open areas between towns maintain regional balance; farms as private property can rob people of biological heritage.
8. **Mosaic of Subcultures** — Cities need diverse co-existing groups, each with their own space.
9. **Scattered Work** — Intermingle work and living zones rather than concentrating them.
10. **Magic of the City** — Put specialized urban magic within reach of everyone.
11. **Local Transport Areas** — Short trips should be inconvenient for cars, encouraging foot/bike travel.
12. **Community of 7,000** — Individuals lose voice in communities over 10k; govern in groups of 5k–10k.
13. **Subculture Boundary** — Different cultures need physical boundaries but shared meeting spaces.
14. **Identifiable Neighborhood** — People need distinct areas they can identify with.
15. **Neighborhood Boundary** — Form boundaries by reducing streets or closing them to through traffic.
16. **Web of Public Transportation** — Transit modes must interconnect; focus on interchanges.
17. **Ring Roads** — High-speed roads should not damage communities or countryside.
18. **Network of Learning** — Emphasize learning over teaching; education through decentralized urban design.
19. **Web of Shopping** — Place shops in gaps where customers exist, away from similar shops.
20. **Mini-Buses** — Small vehicles (up to 6 people) for the final stretch of transit.
21. **Four-story Limit** — Tall buildings offer no real advantages and harm social life, light, and air.
22. **Nine Per Cent Parking** — More than 9% land devoted to parking destroys the environment.
23. **Parallel Roads** — One-way parallel roads improve congestion and reduce cars where children play.
24. **Sacred Sites** — Protect natural/historic landmarks that symbolize an area.
25. **Access to Water** — People are drawn to water; treat it with respect.
26. **Life Cycle** — Each community needs a balance of people at every life stage.
27. **Men and Women** — Every environment needs balance of masculine and feminine.
28. **Eccentric Nucleus** — Place high-density centers at boundaries between subcultures.
29. **Density Rings** — Rings of decreasing density around a community nucleus.
30. **Activity Nodes** — Concentrate community facilities; route paths through them.
31. **Promenade** — Each subculture needs a center for public life where people gather.
32. **Shopping Street** — Shopping needs both traffic access and quiet comfort.
33. **Night Life** — Cluster nighttime venues together for a scene.
34. **Interchange** — Transit interchanges must be extremely convenient.
35. **Household Mix** — Mix household types so people see every life stage face to face.
36. **Degree of Publicness** — Offer housing at three levels: public, private, and in-between.
37. **House Cluster** — Arrange 8–12 houses around shared land.
38. **Row Houses** — Long, thin houses along paths with variation for light.
39. **Housing Hill** — Stepped terraces sloping southward for high-density central areas.
40. **Old People Everywhere** — Old and young need contact with each other.
41. **Work Community** — Workplaces should be communities with courtyards and common areas.
42. **Industrial Ribbon** — Place industry at community boundaries, not isolated by zoning.
43. **University as a Marketplace** — Learning should happen all over town, not in closed institutions.
44. **Local Town Hall** — Each community needs physical governance space near busy intersections.
45. **Necklace of Community Projects** — Surround town halls with small community projects.
46. **Market of Many Shops** — Markets of autonomous, specialized small shops.
47. **Health Center** — Decentralized health centers emphasizing health, not sickness.
48. **Housing in Between** — Build houses into the fabric of shops and services.
49. **Looped Local Roads** — Loops prevent shortcut traffic through neighborhoods.
50. **T Junctions** — Three-way junctions are safer than four-way.
51. **Green Streets** — Plant grass on local roads closed to through traffic.
52. **Network of Paths and Cars** — Pedestrian paths at right angles to roads form a second network.
53. **Main Gateways** — Physical boundaries between town parts.
54. **Road Crossing** — Narrow roads at pedestrian crossings; add islands and canopies.
55. **Raised Walk** — Pedestrian paths ~18 inches above roads near fast traffic.
56. **Bike Paths and Racks** — Designated bike paths within 100 ft of every building.
57. **Children in the City** — Extra-safe bike network parts for children's exploration.
58. **Carnival** — Socially sanctioned activities for outward expression of dreaming.
59. **Quiet Backs** — Quiet natural spaces behind busy buildings.
60. **Accessible Garden** — Green spaces within 3 minutes of every house and workplace.
61. **Small Public Squares** — Short side under 70 ft; ideal is 45–60 ft.
62. **High Places** — Make high points landmarks with physical climbs.
63. **Dancing in the Street** — Raised bandstands in activity pockets.
64. **Pools and Streams** — Expose and preserve all forms of water.
65. **Birth Places** — Local places for childbirth as family/community events.
66. **Holy Ground** — Physical places for life's fundamental rites.
67. **Common Land** — 25% of house cluster land should be common use.
68. **Connected Play** — Connected play spaces away from traffic for children.
69. **Public Outdoor Room** — Partly enclosed outdoor room within view of homes.
70. **Grave Sites** — Small grave sites interspersed throughout the community.
71. **Still Water** — Accessible pools/ponds with natural shallow edges.
72. **Local Sports** — Integrate visible local sports into neighborhoods.
73. **Adventure Playground** — Raw materials spaces where children build their own playgrounds.
74. **Animals** — Access to animals is as important as trees and parks.
75. **The Family** — Extended household of ~12 people; nuclear family doesn't work alone.
76. **House for a Small Family** — Three distinct areas: couple's realm, children's realm, common area.
77. **House for a Couple** — Both shared and private spaces for solitude.
78. **House for One Person** — Simplicity; one central space with nooks.
79. **Your Own Home** — People need ownership/control over their home and garden space.
80. **Self-governing Workshops and Offices** — Small, focused work teams with autonomy.
81. **Small Services without Red Tape** — Large departments lose human qualities.
82. **Office Connections** — Teams more than one floor apart rarely communicate.
83. **Master and Apprentice** — Learning by helping someone who knows what they're doing.
84. **Teen-Age Society** — Spaces for teenagers to explore adult responsibility.
85. **Shopfront Schools** — Small independent schools where kids 7–12 learn by doing.
86. **Children's Home** — Safe places for children with social activities.
87. **Individually Owned Shops** — Small shops keep wealth in the community.
88. **Street Cafe** — Places to sit, relax, and watch people pass by for hours.
89. **Corner Grocery** — Stores as generators of walks.
90. **Beer Hall** — Public gathering places with varied activities, not just bars.
91. **Traveler's Inn** — Small inns with communal spaces for travelers.
92. **Bus Stop** — Tiny centers for public life with seats, shelters, newsstands.
93. **Food Stands** — Owner-operated food stands where cars and paths meet.
94. **Sleeping in Public** — Comfortable semi-sheltered public resting places.

## Patterns 95–204: Buildings

Described as under the control of individuals or small groups, giving "actual 3D shape to buildings."

95. **Building Complex** — Buildings made of smaller cohesive parts, not monolithic.
96. **Number of Stories** — At least 50% of ground area open; similar heights for adjacent buildings.
97. **Shielded Parking** — Hide parking behind natural walls.
98. **Circulation Realms** — Different areas marked by progressively smaller gateways.
99. **Main Building** — A complex needs a center; identify the most essential building.
100. **Pedestrian Street** — No cars except deliveries; buildings open to the street.
101. **Building Thoroughfare** — Indoor "streets" in cold climates, continuous with outdoor streets.
102. **Family of Entrances** — Bold, visible entrances for each building in a complex.
103. **Small Parking Lots** — Space out parking to avoid vast lots.
104. **Site Repair** — Build in areas that need improvement, not only in beautiful areas.
105. **South Facing Outdoors** — Place buildings north of outdoor spaces.
106. **Positive Outdoor Space** — Partly enclosed outdoor spaces are most comfortable.
107. **Wings of Light** — Long, narrow wings for light on two sides.
108. **Connected Buildings** — Buildings leaning against each other force healthy neighborly adaptation.
109. **Long Thin House** — String rooms out for maximum distance between them.
110. **Main Entrance** — Visible from approaching avenues; bold, distinctive shape.
111. **Half-Hidden Garden** — Gardens need privacy with street connection.
112. **Entrance Transition** — Changes of light, sound, direction, or level at entry.
113. **Car Connection** — Shortest route from car to house should be main entrance.
114. **Hierarchy of Open Space** — Protected backs looking out to larger openings.
115. **Courtyards Which Live** — Courtyards with views beyond, multiple doors opening into them.
116. **Cascade of Roofs** — Highest ceilings for most important gathering spaces at center.
117. **Sheltering Roof** — Visible roof vault with low eaves at entrances.
118. **Roof Garden** — At least one small roof garden per building.
119. **Arcades** — Covered walkways at building edges, partly inside and outside.
120. **Paths and Goals** — Paths compatible with walking, with visible goals.
121. **Path Shape** — Bulge in the middle of paths for staying, not just passing.
122. **Building Fronts** — Place buildings up to paths/streets, not set back.
123. **Pedestrian Density** — Under 150 sq ft/person is lively; over 500 feels dead.
124. **Activity Pockets** — Fill edges of squares with activity pockets.
125. **Stair Seats** — Inviting vantage points at stairs.
126. **Something Roughly in the Middle** — A fountain, tree, or statue where paths converge.
127. **Intimacy Gradient** — Sequence from public entrance to most private domains.
128. **Indoor Sunlight** — Most important rooms on the south side (Northern Hemisphere).
129. **Common Areas at the Heart** — Single common area members pass by frequently.
130. **Entrance Room** — Space for arrival, shelter, taking off shoes and coats.
131. **The Flow Through Rooms** — Use rooms for circulation rather than corridors.
132. **Short Passages** — Passages like rooms, with windows on one side.
133. **Staircase as a Stage** — Central visible stair with wide bottom steps.
134. **Zen View** — Glimpse beautiful views in passing; don't make them the main view.
135. **Tapestry of Light and Dark** — Use light to guide people to important places.
136. **Couple's Realm** — Private space for parents separate from children.
137. **Children's Realm** — Continuum of indoor/outdoor play areas separate from couple's realm.
138. **Sleeping to the East** — Bedrooms where people wake with the sun.
139. **Farmhouse Kitchen** — Integrate kitchen work and family activity in one big room.
140. **Private Terrace on the Street** — Common rooms open to a slightly raised street-facing terrace.
141. **A Room of One's Own** — Private space for each family member.
142. **Sequence of Sitting Spaces** — Various degrees of intimacy in corners and alcoves.
143. **Bed Cluster** — Children's beds in alcoves around a common play space.
144. **Bathing Room** — Positive, energizing bathing space with possible outdoor access.
145. **Bulk Storage** — Planned storage for infrequently used items.
146. **Flexible Office Space** — Adapted to specific work but truly flexible.
147. **Communal Eating** — Regular communal meals to hold groups together.
148. **Small Work Groups** — Spatially dedicated teams with shared common areas.
149. **Reception Welcomes You** — Welcoming entrance with soft chairs, food, coffee.
150. **A Place to Wait** — Positive waiting spaces fused with activities.
151. **Small Meeting Rooms** — Majority of meetings for 12 people or fewer.
153. **Rooms to Rent** — Rentable spaces with private entrance and bathroom access.
154. **Teenager's Cottage** — Attached but independent space away from master bedroom.
155. **Old Age Cottage** — Small ground-level cottage nearby for elderly.
156. **Settled Work** — Workspace that grows gradually over a lifetime.
157. **Home Workshop** — Visible from street for interaction.
158. **Open Stairs** — Direct stair access to street for upper-floor apartments.
159. **Light on Two Sides of Every Room** — Natural light from two sides for clear facial expression visibility.
160. **Building Edge** — Edges that invite stopping, sitting, leaning.
161. **Sunny Place** — South-side area for basking, working, playing.
162. **North Face** — Cascade to avoid blocking limited northern sun.
163. **Outdoor Room** — Partly enclosed outdoor space for eating, talking.
164. **Street Windows** — Windows on second and third floors overlooking streets.
165. **Opening to the Street** — Open street-side space with activity visible to passersby.
166. **Gallery Surround** — Balconies and terraces at building edges.
167. **Six-Foot Balcony** — Balconies under 6 ft deep are hardly used.
168. **Connection to the Earth** — Ambiguous boundary between building and earth.
169. **Terraced Slope** — Terraces along contour lines to control erosion.
170. **Fruit Trees** — Small orchards on common land along paths.
171. **Tree Places** — Trees forming enclosures, avenues, squares, groves.
172. **Garden Growing Wild** — Wilderness quality in gardens, tamed but natural.
173. **Garden Wall** — Protect quiet gardens from traffic sounds.
174. **Trellised Walk** — Trellises to protect paths and shape outdoor spaces.
175. **Greenhouse** — Attached greenhouse accessible from inside and outside.
176. **Garden Seat** — Quiet place with direct sun for solitude.
177. **Vegetable Garden** — Central, sunny fenced garden with tool storage.
178. **Compost** — Dry composting chambers reclaiming nutrients.
179. **Alcoves** — Small places at edges of common rooms for one or two people.
180. **Window Place** — Window seats, bay windows, low sills with comfortable chairs.
181. **The Fire** — Natural focus for thinking, talking, dreaming.
182. **Eating Atmosphere** — Heavy table with direct light above, enclosed space.
183. **Workplace Enclosure** — Balance between too enclosed and too exposed.
184. **Cooking Layout** — Counter length and functional relationships matter.
185. **Sitting Circle** — Loose circle formation with paths and activities.
186. **Communal Sleeping** — Occasional shared sleeping area near fire.
187. **Marriage Bed** — Intimate anchor point, slightly enclosed.
188. **Bed Alcove** — Beds in alcoves off non-sleeping rooms.
189. **Dressing Rooms** — Space between bed and bathroom for dressing.
190. **Ceiling Height Variety** — Higher for large gatherings, lower for small, very low for alcoves.
191. **The Shape of Indoor Space** — Rough rectangles with straight walls and nearly symmetrical vaulted ceilings.
192. **Windows Overlooking Life** — Views to activities in streets or gardens.
193. **Half-Open Wall** — Balance of openness and enclosure using columns, half-walls, sliding doors.
194. **Interior Windows** — Windows between rooms to brighten dark spaces.
195. **Staircase Volume** — Stairs making a complete two-story structural bay.
196. **Corner Doors** — Doors near corners to avoid disrupting room activity.
197. **Thick Walls** — Deep walls containing shelves, cabinets, built-in seats.
198. **Closets Between Rooms** — Interior wall closets for acoustic insulation, not exterior walls.
199. **Sunny Counter** — Kitchen counters near big windows for sunlight.
200. **Open Shelves** — Shallow shelves, items one deep.
201. **Waist-High Shelf** — Long, 9–15 inch deep shelves around main rooms.
202. **Built-in Seats** — Test seat position before building.
203. **Child Caves** — Small caves in leftover spaces, low ceiling, tiny entrance.
204. **Secret Place** — Nearly impossible to discover unless shown.

## Patterns 205–253: Construction

How to make buildings, rooms, and spaces in detail.

205. **Structure Follows Social Spaces** — Social functions direct building form, not vice versa.
206. **Efficient Structure** — Load-bearing walls, columns at frequent intervals, vaulted roofs.
207. **Good Materials** — Biodegradable, low-energy, easy-to-modify natural materials over "modern" ones.
208. **Gradual Stiffening** — Start as flimsy frame; stiffen gradually as materials are added.
209. **Roof Layout** — Largest roof over most important communal spaces.
210. **Floor and Ceiling Layout** — Vaults, walls, archways maintaining social space integrity.
211. **Thickening the Outer Walls** — Depth and volume in walls for alcoves, seats, shelves.
212. **Columns at the Corners** — Place columns at room corners from the start.
213. **Final Column Distribution** — Columns furthest apart on ground level, closer higher up.
214. **Root Foundations** — Columns extending into earth like tree roots.
215. **Ground Floor Slab** — Slightly raised slab (6–9 inches) with perimeter wall and fill.
216. **Box Columns** — Hollow tubes with stiff outer skin and solid core.
217. **Perimeter Beams** — Continuous beam around rooms to support vaults.
218. **Wall Membrane** — Inner and outer membrane with fill between.
219. **Floor-Ceiling Vaults** — Vault shape supporting floor above, forming ceiling below.
220. **Roof Vaults** — Cylindrical barrel or pitched roof with convex curve.
221. **Natural Doors and Windows** — Each built according to its place; windows smaller higher up.
222. **Low Sill** — Sills 12–14 inches high for sitting.
223. **Deep Reveals** — Splayed edges at 50–60 degrees for gentle light transition.
224. **Low Doorway** — Some doorways lower or rounded for more deliberate passage.
225. **Frames as Thickened Edges** — Door/window frames as thickenings of wall material.
226. **Column Place** — Free-standing columns as thick as humans, with places to sit.
227. **Column Connections** — Strong continuous curve ~45 degrees where columns meet beams.
228. **Stair Vault** — Curved diagonal vault for stairs.
229. **Duct Space** — Pipes and conduit in ceiling vault triangular space.
230. **Radiant Heat** — Use radiant heat more than convection in gathering rooms.
231. **Dormer Windows** — Habitable space in roof with dormers for light and air.
232. **Roof Caps** — Human touch at roof peak marking where roof meets sky.
233. **Floor Surface** — Hard materials for public areas, soft for private.
234. **Lapped Outside Walls** — Joined materials keeping weather out, easy to patch.
235. **Soft Inside Walls** — Soft plaster with slight give for comfortable touch.
236. **Windows which Open Wide** — Easy-to-reach windows near flowers and paths.
237. **Solid Doors with Glass** — Glazed upper half with acoustic solidness.
238. **Filtered Light** — Light through leaves, vines, lattices.
239. **Small Panes** — Smaller panes create more intense connection to outside.
240. **Half-inch Trim** — Finishing trim covering minor construction variations.
241. **Seat Spots** — Location more important than appearance; face activities.
242. **Front Door Bench** — Bench outside for watching the world go by.
243. **Sitting Wall** — Low, wide walls around natural outdoor areas.
244. **Canvas Roof** — Foldable canvas awnings for partial shade.
245. **Raised Flowers** — Raised flower beds along edges with solid sides for sitting.
246. **Climbing Plants** — Plants trained up sunny walls around openings.
247. **Paving with Cracks** — Stones with 1-inch gaps for grass and moss.
248. **Soft Tile and Brick** — Materials that wear with time, showing marks of use.
249. **Ornament** — Simple themes applied to boundaries, edges, transitions.
250. **Warm Colors** — Natural wood, sunlight, yellows, reds, oranges for warmth.
251. **Different Chairs** — Variety of chair types instead of identical ones.
252. **Pools of Light** — Low, separated lights forming pools with dark in between.
253. **Things From Your Life** — Decor from your own life tells your story.
