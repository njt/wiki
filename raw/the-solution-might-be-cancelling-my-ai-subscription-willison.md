Simon Willison's link-blog response to David Wilson's piece of the same title at thoughts.hmmz.org, also drawing on the Hacker News discussion. Posted on simonwillison.net, tagged: productivity, ai, generative-ai, llms, coding-agents, ai-misuse.

## Summary

Willison finds Wilson's experience deeply relatable. He reports that coding agents can take him from a vague idea to a working solution — complete with tests and documentation, resembling a weeks-long project — "in less than an hour." His key concern is **maintenance burden**: even if the code is solid, there's a limit to how many such projects he can sensibly care for.

## Key Arguments

### Willison's take
- Coding agents create a genuine maintenance crisis: you can create faster than you can responsibly own.
- "Discipline is the critical skill to develop here" — one he admits he's been trying to figure out "for decades."
- If projects are instantly abandoned, what value did their creation hold?

### Wilson's conclusion (via Willison)
- AI is "a tool producing a cheap reward with minimal input and no friction" that "can only be a liability."
- Realizing this "is probably the only real contribution of AI to date."

### Hacker News counterpoints
- Some commenters with ADHD report finishing side projects for the "first time ever" because they can get them working before getting bored.
- Others describe AI as "a salve for my mind" — maintaining inbox zero and feeling like they "have a support team for the first time."
- A commenter noted that for people prone to hyperfocus, "working with AI can provide the kinds of stimulation we crave," describing feeling more engaged, productive, and "badass."

## Themes

Productivity paradox, attention and focus, ADHD/neurodivergence and AI, discipline as meta-skill, quality vs. volume, maintenance burden.
