## Summary (from web search)

Blake Smith, a historian and translator based in Chicago, examines the "Silicon Valley Canon" — a set of books widely read and discussed by tech leaders such as Patrick Collison, Brian Armstrong, Marc Andreessen, and others.

### The Canon

Books include: *Atlas Shrugged*, *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance*, *The Lord of the Rings*, *Neuromancer*, *Snow Crash*, *The Sovereign Individual*, *Godel, Escher, Bach*, and biographies of Steve Jobs, Elon Musk, and John D. Rockefeller.

### Central Argument

The canon doesn't directly teach tech or investing skills. Instead it reflects and shapes a worldview rooted in long-standing American dilemmas about freedom, power, and individual agency versus systemic forces. It balances books that inspire personal ambition (biographies of titans) with works that humble that ambition by emphasizing large, impersonal structures of power.

Silicon Valley is an outlier in contemporary America because it is one of the last places where people still possess the agency to act on the ambitions and contradictions embedded in classic American mythology — a capacity that most other sectors of society have largely lost.

### Critical Observation

The canon's books are "first encountered mostly in adolescence — and particularly by teenage boys," hinting at a certain arrested development in the culture it fosters.
