# Seeing Is Not Believing: Realistic AI Videos Disrupt Confidence in Authentic Videos and Perceived Reality

## Authors
Eitan Wolf, Yasith Samaradivakara, Om Gokhale, Sally Ahmed, Yuhan Wang, Pat Pataranutaporn, and Pattie Maes

## Venue
Published in *Extended Abstracts of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (CHI EA '26), ACM. DOI: 10.1145/3772363.3798358

## Abstract

Visual media has traditionally served as a reference for what feels real, but generative video models now produce synthetic footage resembling real-world scenes. This content increasingly appears in short-form social feeds where AI-generated and human-generated videos are consumed in rapid succession. Prior work has focused on detecting synthetic content, while less is understood about how such exposure shapes perception of subsequent human-generated media.

## Methodology

A two-phase study with N = 100 participants was conducted. The study compared:
- An AI-exposure group — participants first exposed to AI-generated videos
- A control group — participants viewing human-generated videos

After this initial phase, both groups transitioned to human-generated content for the second phase.

Key design feature: AI-generated videos were accompanied by clear disclosure that they were synthetic.

## Key Findings

Despite disclosure that the AI videos were synthetic, the AI-exposure group reported:

1. Increased doubt about the authenticity of subsequent videos
2. Reduced judgment confidence regarding whether videos were real or AI-generated
3. Greater perceptual disruption
4. Lower social connectedness

## Conclusions

Perceptual and psychological effects occur even when synthetic content is disclosed. The authors call for design strategies prioritizing "perceptual safety" — addressing experiential impacts beyond deception.
